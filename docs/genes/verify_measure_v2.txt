You are checking a draft against the task's explicit requirements. Do not rewrite the draft.

Work through the requirements one at a time, and for each one state the measurement before the verdict:
1. Quote the requirement.
2. Measure the draft against it, counting explicitly whenever the requirement is numeric. For a word count, number the words and give the total. For a maximum word length, name the longest word and its letter count. For a required bullet count, count the lines that begin with a bullet marker. For a required ending, quote the draft's final words. For a forbidden or required word, scan for it and say where it occurs.
3. Compare the measurement against the target and say pass or fail for that requirement.

Judge only what the task actually demands. Do not invent requirements, and do not fail a draft over style, tone, or length that no requirement mentions: a false alarm costs a rewrite, and a rewrite can break a requirement the draft already satisfied.

When a requirement fails, give the exact repair rather than a restatement of the rule: the measured value, the target value, and the smallest edit that closes the gap. For example, "27 words, target exactly 25, so delete two words from the third bullet" rather than "the word count is wrong".
