\begin{abstract}
Reflective prompt optimizers such as GEPA \citep{agrawal2025gepa} can evolve \emph{multiple}
text components of a compound LLM system, not just a single prompt, and a natural hypothesis
is that optimizing more of a generate--critique--refine loop should beat optimizing its
generation prompt alone. On a corrected, control-heavy harness what the optimizer \emph{does} to
the verifier is governed by one number, and what that costs is smaller than we first reported.
Write $p_0$ for the base rate of first drafts that already satisfy every requirement. At
$p_0 = 0.067$ the reflective search converges on a verifier that never accepts anything, on
$11$ of $12$ evolved genes across four training-split sizes; at $p_0 = 0.267$, holding harness,
optimizer, budget and seeds fixed and changing only which constraints the task generator may
use, evolving the verifier alongside the generator \emph{gains} $0.060$ held-out over evolving
the generator alone, with all three seeds agreeing. The treatment demonstrably reached the
verifier at every level --- GEPA rewrote it on three of three seeds, producing detector rubrics
of ${\sim}3000$ characters against a $127$-character seed.

We also report, and withdraw, the negative half of our own headline. An earlier draft read the
degenerate verifier as \emph{costing} held-out performance at low $p_0$
($\dd(D-B) = -0.042$). A pre-registered sweep over the training split, at fixed $p_0$, fixed
held-out tasks and fixed seeds, returns $+0.017$, $-0.042$, $+0.062$, $+0.023$ at
$n_{\mathrm{train}} \in \{4,8,16,24\}$: three of four positive, the lone negative cell being the
original run. Five released runs happen to re-score one fixed seed genome on one fixed held-out
set, which recovers the sampling variance no interval in this study contained,
$\sigma(\dd) = 0.028$ on a thirty-task mean --- and puts that $-0.042$ at $1.49\sigma$. So the
degenerate verifier is real and robust while its cost is not established, and the two claims
are separable in a way we had run together. The refine accounting shows why they can come
apart: a never-accepting verifier is behaviourally \emph{always refine}, and where nearly every
draft is wrong that is close to the right policy.

The mechanism explains the verifier's collapse, and predicted it before we paid. Unconditional
refinement is
nearly free on a training signal where almost nothing is already correct, and costly where much
of it is, so the search converges on a non-accepting verifier exactly where $p_0$ is low.
Measured: the evolved critique emits \texttt{PASS} zero times on every seed at $p_0 = 0.067$,
and on two of three seeds at $p_0 = 0.667$ --- and it never emits a false \texttt{PASS} at any
level, against six from the seed rubric it is compared with, so what the search acquires is
precision-1 acceptance rather than looser acceptance. This is Goodhart's law inside the loop,
and it is cheap to screen for, since the failure shows up as a \texttt{PASS} rate of zero. We
also report what the mechanism gets wrong, because the released artifact is enough to check it:
since both arms evolve the shared generation gene, $\dd$ is a whole-system contrast, and
decomposing it attributes $60\%$ of the $-0.042$ to that shared gene and $100\%$ of the $+0.060$
to the treatment --- which is a second reason the $-0.042$ cannot carry a claim about the
treatment, independent of its size relative to noise. Stratifying on a pre-treatment covariate
then shows the loss does \emph{not}
sit where an always-refine account puts it --- the already-correct drafts contribute $+0.002$ ---
so we withdraw that half of our own explanation while the training-signal half, which is what
predicted the sign, stands. Separately, the
intrinsic critique is a near-random detector, consistent with the documented unreliability of
LLM self-verification \citep{huang2023cannot,kamoi2024whencan}; across a $10\times$ range of
$p_0$ its $\mathrm{F}_1$ nearly triples while Youden's $J$ and Matthews' correlation stay at or
below chance, so a rising $\mathrm{F}_1$ on an easier benchmark is base-rate inflation and not a
better detector. Detector quality is also not sufficient: a \emph{perfect} inference-time
checker yields the \emph{worst} of three held-out scores. Because several headline numbers are
nulls we also report a positive control: on a landscape where evolving the critique is the only
path to a passing answer, the same harness and optimizer return $\dd(D-B)=+1.000$.

Reaching that measurement required discarding every contrast run we had bought before it ---
fifteen of them. An audit
established that a component-agnostic reflective dataset and a one-component-per-proposal
selector had together prevented the loop-level arm from ever receiving its intervention: the
critique gene is byte-identical to the seed in $15/15$ winning genomes. Because such a defect
is invisible in held-out scores, we propose a reporting minimum --- \textbf{per component,
whether the optimizer's output differs from the seed} --- and it is not hypothetical:
\citet{pappu2026multiagent} independently report GEPA returning the seed prompt unchanged on
two benchmarks. Appendix~\ref{sec:defect} documents the failure modes; we release the
corrected harness, every cassette, and every pre-registration, the superseded runs labelled
as such.
\end{abstract}
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Automatic prompt optimization (APO) has matured from single-instruction search
\citep{madaan2023selfrefine} to optimizers that treat an entire LLM program as
the object of optimization. GEPA \citep{agrawal2025gepa}, the current
state-of-the-art reflective optimizer and an ICLR-2026 oral, evolves any set of
text components of a compound system --- prompts, rubrics, tool descriptions ---
by reflecting on execution traces in natural language and maintaining a Pareto
frontier of candidates. DSPy \citep{khattab2023dspy} similarly compiles and
optimizes multi-module pipelines end-to-end.

This capability raises a concrete engineering question that, to our knowledge,
has not been isolated experimentally for a self-refine loop's own verifier. The
adjacent question for multi-\emph{agent} systems has since been benchmarked:
\citet{bai2026masprompt} find that GEPA improves single-agent performance on all five of
their tasks ($+2$ to $+14$ points) while its multi-agent extension ranges from $-16$ to
$+14$ depending on task and workflow topology --- more optimizable surface is not reliably
better. Our question is narrower and about a different object: \emph{given a self-refine loop, does
optimizing the loop's verification/critique component (and beyond) yield better
held-out performance than optimizing only its generation prompt, under a matched
budget?} We call the former \textbf{loop-level} and the latter
\textbf{prompt-only} optimization. The hypothesis is intuitive --- more
optimizable surface, more headroom --- and if true it would justify the extra
cost of evolving critique rubrics and plan modules.


We set out to answer this question with a pre-registered, control-heavy study. Our
contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The measurement, and the condition it depends on} (\S\ref{sec:results}): under
    a matched budget on a corrected harness, loop-level optimization does not beat prompt-only
    optimization at low $p_0$, the base rate of already-correct first drafts, with the treatment
    verified to have reached the gene on every seed. Holding harness, optimizer, budget and
    seeds fixed and changing only which constraints the task generator may use raises $p_0$ from
    $0.067$ to $0.267$, and the comparison turns to $+0.060$ with all three seeds agreeing
    (\S\ref{sec:p0}). That is the study's largest loop-level effect and the one that replicates.
    A first draft of this paper also reported the low-$p_0$ cell as a \emph{cost} of $0.042$;
    a pre-registered split-size sweep and an offline estimate of the study's own measurement
    noise together withdraw that, and \S\ref{sec:splitresult} reports the withdrawal rather than
    burying it. What the condition governs, robustly, is what the optimizer does to the
    verifier; what it costs is smaller than we could measure.
  \item \textbf{The mechanism} (\S\ref{sec:analysis}): the optimizer converges on a verifier
    that never accepts. Scored as a detector, the evolved gene emits \texttt{PASS} zero times
    on every seed at low $p_0$ and loses the one true positive the seed rubric had.
    Unconditional refinement is near-free on a training signal where most first drafts fail,
    so this is Goodhart's law operating inside the loop --- and it is screenable, before
    spending, as a \texttt{PASS} rate of zero. As $p_0$ rises the same search learns to accept,
    and does so at \emph{precision~1}: it never emits a false \texttt{PASS} at any level, while
    the seed rubric it is compared against emits six.
  \item \textbf{How much of a componentized $\dd$ is the component} (\S\ref{sec:decomp}):
    because both arms evolve the shared \texttt{generate} gene, $\dd(D-B)$ is a whole-system
    contrast and not a measurement of the treatment. Splitting it into a draft term and a loop
    term --- an identity, computable offline from the released artifact --- shows the $-0.042$
    is $60\%$ shared gene while the $+0.060$ is $100\%$ treatment. It also refutes the
    outcome-side half of our own mechanism: the stratum the always-\texttt{FIX} account blames
    contributes $+0.002$, not the deficit.
  \item \textbf{A mechanistic account of the ceiling}: detection quality is necessary but not
    sufficient (a perfect checker gives the worst of three scores); \emph{given} a perfect
    checker, repair-feedback \emph{shape} is worth $+0.063$ (text) and $+0.20$ (code) while
    naming the failed constraint adds nothing; and the residual localizes to a single
    mechanical skill rather than a diffuse ceiling.
  \item \textbf{A positive control} (\S\ref{sec:posctrl}), because every headline here is a
    null: on a landscape where the critique gene is the only path to a passing answer, the
    same harness and optimizer return $\dd(D-B) = +1.000$.
  \item A taxonomy separating a legitimate non-oracle \emph{measurement tool} from oracle
    contamination (\S\ref{sec:method}), which a pre-spend design review found essential: two
    seemingly-innocuous statistics were the scoring oracle in disguise.
  \item \textbf{A silent failure mode for componentized optimization, and the one-integer
    diagnostic that catches it} (Appendix~\ref{sec:defect}). We establish it on our own
    artifact: pointing an optimizer at a component is not the same as optimizing it, and the
    difference does not appear in held-out scores. Our first fifteen contrast runs measured
    $\dd$ from an arm that never received its treatment --- ten of them exactly $0$, the rest
    moved by a confound in the gene the arms \emph{share}.
\end{enumerate}

We report every result as-is, including a run in which optimization \emph{degraded} held-out
performance, and including the audit that invalidated our own first headline. The point is not
that GEPA is weak --- it is a strong optimizer --- but that \emph{pointing an optimizer at a
component is not the same as optimizing it}.
\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Loop engineering, and which loop this paper is about.}
During June 2026 industry practice converged on a name for designing the machinery
\emph{around} a model rather than its prompt. Steinberger compressed it into a slogan ---
stop prompting coding agents, design the loops that prompt them
\citep{steinberger2026loops} --- Osmani's essay named the practice \emph{loop engineering}
\citep{osmani2026loop}, and Swyx framed the general form as \emph{loopcraft}, ``the art of
stacking loops'' \citep{swyx2026loopcraft}. The stack is commonly decomposed into four
loops \citep{langchain2026loopcraft}:

\begin{description}
  \item[L1 --- Agent loop.] The model calls tools repeatedly until the task is done.
    \emph{Out of scope here}: our worker is a fixed generate--critique--refine sequence with
    no tool-calling agent.
  \item[L2 --- Verification loop.] The output is scored against a rubric and retried with
    feedback if it fails. \textbf{This is our object of study.} The \texttt{ensure\_correct}
    gene \emph{is} the verifier, the PASS gate plus \texttt{loop\_max\_retries} \emph{is}
    the stop rule, and our fix-budget sweep ($R{:}1\!\to\!3$) is a stop-rule experiment.
  \item[L3 --- Event-driven loop.] External events trigger agent runs inside a larger
    system. \emph{Out of scope}: our loop is invoked once per benchmark task, so we study no
    triggers, schedules, or cross-task memory.
  \item[L4 --- Hill-climbing loop.] Execution traces feed an analysis step that improves the
    harness configuration --- called ``arguably most important''
    \citep{langchain2026loopcraft}. \textbf{This is our intervention.} GEPA reflects on
    recorded traces and rewrites the loop's own text components; arm~D is an L4 loop pointed
    at an L2 loop.
\end{description}

So the paper's question, in this vocabulary, is: \emph{can a hill-climbing loop improve a
verification loop by rewriting its verifier?} ``Loop-level'' throughout means the verifier
and its stop rule --- not the full stack --- and we vary topology only through an optional
\texttt{plan} module and an optional repair \texttt{actuator}.

Three consequences for how we read our own results. First, this framing is what makes the
defect in Section~\ref{sec:defect} worth a paper rather than a bug report: the failure is
not GEPA-specific but a hazard for \emph{any} L4 loop, because attributing trace evidence to
the wrong L2 component optimizes the wrong thing while the scores stay plausible. Second,
Osmani's central prescription for L2 is a \emph{separated} verifier --- ``the model that
wrote the code is way too nice grading its own homework''
\citep{osmani2026loop} --- which is a qualitative claim our design measures directly: the
intrinsic critique scores $\mathrm{F}_1\!\approx\!0.11$ as a failure detector
(\S\ref{sec:detector}, where we also show why $\mathrm{F}_1$ is the wrong statistic for it and
that the base-rate-independent ones put it at chance), and our cross-lineage critic run tests
the separation remedy.
Third, chronology: our runs were pre-registered and executed around and before these posts
appeared, so the correspondence is convergent rather than causal --- the practitioner
literature supplied the vocabulary and the stakes, not the hypotheses.

\paragraph{Reflective and evolutionary prompt optimization.}
GEPA \citep{agrawal2025gepa} reports beating GRPO by up to $20\%$ with $35\times$
fewer rollouts and exceeding MIPROv2 by over $10\%$. Crucially for us, GEPA
optimizes \emph{one or more} components and is known to plateau. The sharpest published
instance is independent of us: optimizing a prompt with GEPA across six tasks,
\citet{pappu2026multiagent} report that ``on GPQA Diamond and MATH-500, GEPA returned the
original prompt essentially unchanged.'' That is our failure mode observed in someone
else's pipeline, and reported --- which is the practice \S\ref{sec:defect} argues should be
routine. OPRO \citep{yang2023opro} and EvoPrompt \citep{guo2024evoprompt} likewise use an
LLM as the optimizer, and OPRO's authors quantify the over-fitting risk directly: their
``training accuracies are often 5\%--20\% higher than our test accuracies''. Our null is a
member of this ``plateau/degrade'' family, isolated under controlled conditions.

\paragraph{Self-refine and the limits of self-correction.}
Self-Refine \citep{madaan2023selfrefine} popularized generate--critique--refine
with a single model, reporting $\sim\!20\%$ gains on generation-quality tasks.
Huang et al.\ \citep{huang2023cannot} show the sharp boundary: \emph{intrinsic}
self-correction (no external feedback) fails to improve and often degrades
(CommonSenseQA $75.8 \!\to\! 38.1$ after one round, recovering only to $41.8$ after a
second), and prior positive results
relied on \emph{oracle} stopping signals that ``vanish when oracle labels are
not available.'' Self-correction helps only with a genuine external verification
mechanism (a tool, a code executor, unit tests). A parallel line shows LLM
self-verifiers are unreliable detectors --- high false-positive rates
\citep{valmeekam2023selfcritique} and a self-enhancement bias, with cross-family
verification outperforming self-verification. These results predict our loop-level
null and motivate our feedback-tool and cross-verifier directions.

\paragraph{When does optimization help?}
Recent work explicitly frames the boundary question for multi-agent systems and
cost-aware settings. We adopt the same posture for the narrower loop-level vs.\
prompt-only question and contribute a pre-registered answer with controls.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{The loop.}
The worker is a real generate--critique--refine loop. A \texttt{generate} gene
drafts an answer; a \texttt{ensure\_correct} (verify) gene drives an LLM
self-critique that returns \texttt{PASS} or a list of fixes and gates a bounded
number of refine rounds; the final answer is scored by a programmatic metric.
Critically, the critique is \emph{intrinsic}: the programmatic scorer is never
consulted inside the loop (that would be oracle contamination), so the loop
cannot game its own metric. Each eval costs $1 + 2R$ LLM calls for $R$ refine
rounds (or $2 + 2R$ with the optional plan module below).

\paragraph{Four arms.}
All arms share an identical seed genome; they differ only in what GEPA may
evolve, so a held-out gap isolates componentized optimization from a
``more prompt text'' confound.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A} --- seed baseline, evolves nothing.
  \item \textbf{B} --- prompt-only: evolves the \texttt{generate} gene.
  \item \textbf{C} --- command control: evolves the whole genome but scores with
    a genome-blind worker; its landscape is flat by construction (a harness
    soundness check).
  \item \textbf{D} --- loop-level: evolves the whole genome
    (\texttt{generate}\,$+$\,\texttt{ensure\_correct}, and \texttt{plan} when
    enabled) with the LLM worker.
\end{itemize}
$\dd = \mathrm{mean}(D) - \mathrm{mean}(B)$ is the quantity of interest.
A and C are expected flat. \textbf{Caveat, established after the fact}
(\S\ref{sec:defect}): as originally implemented neither control could fail --- A copied its
own seed score with $\delta$ hardcoded to $0.0$, and C's command loop scored a saturated
$1.0$, so it proposed nothing. The corrected harness re-measures A and gives C a
genome-blind constant draft scored by the real metric, which is flat without being
saturated.

\paragraph{Optimizer.}
We drive the official GEPA \citep{agrawal2025gepa} via a loop-genome adapter, calling
\texttt{gepa.optimize} with \texttt{seed\_candidate}, \texttt{trainset}, \texttt{adapter},
\texttt{reflection\_lm} and \texttt{max\_metric\_calls}, plus a
\texttt{MaxCandidateProposalsStopper} to make the reflection-LM spend a hard cap. GEPA's reflection channel (offline, between
training iterations) sees per-constraint feedback prose --- this is the standard
optimizer signal and is identical across B and D; it is distinct from the
in-loop critique gate.

\paragraph{Two levers.}
(i) \emph{Feedback richness}: the critique step is optionally given
\texttt{text\_stats(draft)}, a deterministic one-line readout of measured
properties of the draft (word/character/sentence/bullet counts, longest-word
length, last words). It takes only the text and never sees the task, so it is a
\emph{measurement}, the tool/executor signal \citep{huang2023cannot} found
effective --- not the oracle. (ii) \emph{Complexity}: an optional third
\texttt{plan} gene turns the loop into plan--generate--critique--refine, giving
D a third interacting module to co-tune (the DSPy lever); B still evolves only
\texttt{generate}.

\paragraph{Oracle taxonomy (a required distinction).}
Structural non-oracle-ness (the tool takes no task argument) is necessary but
\textbf{not sufficient}. Our first rule was that a statistic is \emph{informationally} the
oracle when its verifier has no per-task parameter, so the raw measurement alone resolves the
verdict. A pre-spend design review used it to remove \texttt{all\_lowercase} and a comma count,
whose parameter-free verdicts the measurement would simply have been. That rule is too weak,
and we replace it with a three-way distinction:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
\item a \textbf{scoring oracle}: the held-out metric itself, never admissible in the loop;
\item a \textbf{verdict-equivalent measurement}: admissible and deployable, but it settles the
verdict by comparison rather than judgement, because the task statement supplies the target
and the tool supplies the measured value;
\item a \textbf{non-determining measurement}: maps to no verifier, or leaves the verdict open.
\end{itemize}
Every retained field passes the old rule, since each needs a task-supplied target. Under the
new one, four of the eight hard-constraint families are verdict-equivalent once the task
statement is in the prompt: \texttt{words} against \texttt{exact\_word\_count},
\texttt{longest\_word\_letters} against \texttt{word\_max\_length}, \texttt{bullet\_lines}
against \texttt{num\_bullets}, \texttt{last\_words} against \texttt{end\_with}. That is $165$
of $288$ constraint instances ($57\%$) in the hard pool, with at least one on every one of the
$64$ tasks --- though only $2$ tasks ($3\%$) are settled by the tool alone, since
\texttt{forbidden\_word} and \texttt{include\_keyword} are uncovered. This does not contaminate
held-out scores, which never see the tool. What it changes is the reading of the detector
result in \S\ref{sec:detector}: the tool-fed critique's $\mathrm{F}_1$ of $0.11 \to 0.83$ is
substantially the tool \emph{replacing} the detector on the majority of constraint checks,
rather than a prompt that learned to detect better.

\paragraph{Reproducibility and budget.}
Every LLM call is recorded to a content-addressed cassette (\texttt{sha256} of
name$+$args), so runs replay offline at zero cost and partial spend is always
persisted. Reproduction is a first-class, strict operation: re-running any study
with \texttt{-{}-replay} re-executes the entire pipeline (loaders, GEPA search,
scoring) against the cached calls with no API key, and any cassette miss raises --- per call,
and, after the correction in Evidence~7, per run as well, since a consumer that swallows the
per-call error can otherwise let a replay finish with a different number.
GEPA is deterministic under replay, so replay is bit-for-bit:
\texttt{scripts/verify\_reproducibility.py} registers twenty committed runs. Twelve are
labelled \textbf{superseded} (\S\ref{sec:defect}) --- the ten of
Appendix~\ref{sec:superseded}, the MBPP+ attempt of \S\ref{sec:bcb}, and the discarded first
four-arm attempt --- because the corrected harness changes every reflection prompt and
therefore every cassette key, so it cannot replay them and the script reports them as skipped
rather than verified. The remaining eight were recorded under the corrected harness and every
per-arm held-out score replays exactly: the two hand-written-verifier conditions of
\S\ref{sec:analysis}, the four-arm re-run of \S\ref{sec:rerun}, the two new $p_0$ levels
of \S\ref{sec:p0}, and the three bought rows of the split sweep (\S\ref{sec:splitresult}).
Output tokens are hard-capped via enforced \texttt{max\_tokens};
a pre-flight guard refuses any run whose (conservative) estimate exceeds
\texttt{-{}-max-usd}, whose default is \$5; four runs raised it explicitly, at three
distinct values (\$6.18 twice, \$5.89, \$11.76), so \$5 is a default rather than a study-wide
ceiling. Every experiment was pre-registered --- hypothesis, frozen
configuration, expected seed band, and verdict rule committed to version control
\emph{before} spending.

\paragraph{Verdict rule (pre-registered).}
\textbf{SUPPORTED} iff $\dd > \sigma_B + \sigma_D$ and positive;
\textbf{NOT SUPPORTED} iff $\mathrm{mean}(D) \le \mathrm{mean}(B)$;
\textbf{INCONCLUSIVE} if positive-but-within-noise, fewer than two seeds, or a
ceiling/floor flag fires (seed held-out $\ge 0.9$ or $\le 0.1$). This is a
deliberately conservative bar (the sum of two standard deviations), erring
toward under-claiming.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Models.} Worker \texttt{gpt-4.1-mini} (output cap 320 tokens); reflection
\texttt{gpt-5.6-luna} on every run recorded under the corrected harness --- the four-arm
re-run, the $p_0$ sweep and the split-size sweep --- and \texttt{gpt-5.6-terra}, which is the
CLI default, on the superseded runs that preceded it (output cap 1500 throughout). The
reflection model is therefore \emph{not} constant across the superseded and corrected sets, so
it is one more reason not to read a superseded number against a corrected one; within each set
it is fixed, which is what the contrasts require.

\paragraph{Tasks.} A composable IFEval-style \citep{zhou2023ifeval}
multi-constraint generator with nine independent, parameter-carrying verifiers
(e.g.\ \texttt{exact\_word\_count}, \texttt{forbidden\_word},
\texttt{word\_max\_length}, \texttt{num\_bullets}). Joint satisfiability of every
generated task is guaranteed by a witness the generator validates to score
$1.0$ (verified exhaustively: $3{,}189$ easy-tier and $19{,}632$ hard-tier
combinations). The \emph{hard} tier mandates \texttt{exact\_word\_count} (a
genuine LLM skill gap) plus at least one further ``brutal'' constraint, which
structurally caps the seed below saturation.

\paragraph{Metric.} Mean fraction of constraints satisfied on a disjoint
held-out split (\texttt{mc\_score} $\in [0,1]$), scored programmatically (no LLM
judge). We report a relative result (D vs.\ B under matched budget), not a
leaderboard number.


\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

We report the four-arm contrast first (\S\ref{sec:rerun}), then the loop's ceiling
independently of any optimizer: how good the critique is as a detector and what happens when
it is made perfect (\S\ref{sec:detector}), which fix-side levers pay (\S\ref{sec:fixside}),
and where the residual sits (\S\ref{sec:residual}). A cross-domain replication on frontier-hard
code follows (\S\ref{sec:bcb}). The last two subsections are the ones that change how the
first should be read: \S\ref{sec:p0} varies $p_0$ and finds the contrast's sign is conditional
on it, and \S\ref{sec:decomp} separates the part of that contrast which is actually the
treatment from the part contributed by the gene both arms share. The ceiling results come from the arm that
scores a \emph{fixed} genome, so they are independent of the optimizer wiring and of the
defect discussed in Appendix~\ref{sec:defect}; fifteen earlier contrast runs are superseded and
the ten pre-registered ones are recorded, with the reason, in Appendix~\ref{sec:superseded}.

\subsection{The four-arm re-run on the corrected harness}
\label{sec:rerun}

The one four-arm contrast in this paper that measures what the study set out to measure was bought on a rebuilt harness, after the audit of Appendix~\ref{sec:defect} and under the prediction registered in \S\ref{sec:directions} and reproduced verbatim there. Same task family, budget and worker as run~3
(multi-hard, $\mathrm{mmc}=28$, 8 train / 10 held-out, one refine round, three seeds), with
the three corrections in place: a component-specific reflective dataset, an explicit
\texttt{module\_selector=all}, and a non-saturating arm~C.

We bought it twice: a first attempt was discarded because an empty reflection completion was
accepted as the verify gene, so the arm carrying the treatment ran with no critique at all
(Appendix~\ref{sec:defect}, defect~8). Both runs are released; everything below is the clean
re-run.

\textbf{The treatment was applied.} Arm~D's \texttt{genes\_changed} lists
\texttt{ensure\_correct} on \textbf{three of three} seeds, against $0/5$ for arm~D on the
superseded flagship run and $0/15$ pooling all its arms (Appendix~\ref{sec:superseded}). The genes GEPA wrote are substantial detector rubrics of $3235$,

not its width but its content: the arms produced different genomes and different scores, so
the interval measures a contrast that exists.

The result we did not predict is Table~\ref{tab:rerun}'s last column. The two optimizing arms
did \emph{not} behave alike: \textbf{prompt-only optimization is a wash} ($+0.025$, $0.000$,
$-0.025$; mean exactly $0.000$) while \textbf{loop-level optimization costs} $-0.042$
($-0.070$, $-0.080$, $+0.025$). We read that, at the time, as small-split overfitting: eight
training tasks against ten held-out, with arm~D carrying two genes of surface to overfit rather
than one. Three later results dismantle the reading, and the paragraph is left standing so the
correction is legible rather than quietly absorbed. \S\ref{sec:decomp} attributes $-0.025$ of
the $-0.042$ to the gene both arms evolve. The pre-registered split sweep
(\S\ref{sec:splitresult}) finds no split-size trend to overfit along: a \emph{smaller} split
gives $+0.017$ and larger ones $+0.062$ and $+0.023$, all at the same $p_0$ and the same
held-out tasks. And the noise floor (\S\ref{sec:threats}) puts this cell at $1.49\sigma$. The
degradation runs 4 and 5 showed, and the reported instability of prompt optimizers on some tasks
\citep{yang2023opro,zhou2025bbopromptscale}, remain the relevant context; what they no longer
support is a directional claim from this cell. So the honest summary of loop-level optimization
here is not that it fails to help, and no longer that it pays a price: it is that this is the
one configuration of four where it came out negative, by an amount this study cannot separate
from measurement noise.

We flag one earlier reading we withdraw. The contaminated first attempt showed \emph{both}
arms falling below baseline, and we described that as optimization costing something
generally. On the clean re-run arm~B is exactly flat, so that generalization does not
survive; the cost is specific to the arm with the extra gene.

\paragraph{Where the contrast lives.}
Because every run now releases per-task held-out vectors, the seed-level means can be opened
up. Arms~B and~D score the same ten held-out tasks per seed, so the contrast decomposes into
task flips: $5/10$ tasks differ on seed~0 (B better on 4, D on 1), $4/10$ on seed~1 (B better
on 3, D on 1), and $2/10$ on seed~2 (D better on 2, B on 0). Pooled, $19$ of $30$ task scores
are exact ties and the entire $\dd$ rests on eleven flips, $7$ favouring B and $4$ favouring
D. We report this as description, not as extra power: the tasks
are nested within seeds, since one training split produces the genome pair that scores all
ten, so the inferential unit remains the seed and $n$ is still $3$.

Arm~C is worth one qualification, because its number is unchanged from the retracted runs and
what it demonstrates is not. Previously it scored a saturated $1.0$, so GEPA saw a perfect
program and proposed \emph{nothing}: no part of the search ran. Here it starts at $0.23$ to
$0.36$ and GEPA proposes four candidates on every seed, evaluates them, and accepts none. The
flatness
is still true by construction --- a genome-blind worker's training score cannot improve, so
acceptance is impossible --- but the control now exercises the machinery it is supposed to
check instead of exiting before it starts.


\subsection{Detector quality, and the non-monotonic ceiling}
\label{sec:detector}

\paragraph{The critique is an unreliable detector --- measured.} The
mechanism by which a better verify gene \emph{could} help is by catching failures
the generator cannot self-see and steering refinement. We measure the in-loop
critique directly as a binary classifier of ``the draft satisfies all
constraints,'' computed offline from the recorded cassettes (zero cost) by
pairing each critique's \texttt{PASS}/\texttt{FAIL} verdict with the ground-truth
\texttt{mc\_pass} of the exact draft it judged, on the seed loop over the
held-out split (Table~\ref{tab:curve}, left columns). The intrinsic critique is a
\emph{near-random} detector: F$_1 = 0.11$ on run~3 and effectively $0$ on run~5
(it emits \texttt{PASS} on drafts that fail, precision $0.08$). A detector this
poor offers no reliable signal to sharpen, so evolving its rubric cannot
systematically improve outcomes --- the direct mechanism of the loop-level null.
Strikingly, the \emph{same} critique fed the measurement tool (run~4) becomes a
reliable detector (precision $1.00$, F$_1 = 0.83$) --- which is precisely why the
tool lifts the baseline (Section~\ref{sec:results}); but that reliable detector
sits in \emph{both} the B and D loops, so it does not open a loop-level gap. This
matches the documented unreliability of LLM self-verification --- high
false-positive rates, self-enhancement bias, cross-family verifiers beating
self-verification \citep{valmeekam2023selfcritique,huang2023cannot}.


\paragraph{$\mathrm{F}_1$ is the wrong statistic, and the $p_0$ calibration shows it.}
Preparing the sweep of \S\ref{sec:p0} produced three task distributions differing only in which
constraints the generator may use, with measured first-draft pass rates $p_0$ of $0.067$,
$0.267$ and $0.667$. Scoring the \emph{same} seed critique as a detector across all three
separates two things that $\mathrm{F}_1$ conflates.

$\mathrm{F}_1$ nearly triples as the tasks get easier, and it is measuring the base rate, not
the detector: Youden's $J$ and Matthews' correlation --- both of which are zero for a
chance-level detector regardless of base rate --- do not improve at all, and at $L_3$ they are
\emph{exactly} zero because $\mathrm{tp}\cdot\mathrm{tn} = \mathrm{fp}\cdot\mathrm{fn} = 48$.
Across a $10\times$ range of task difficulty the intrinsic critique never rises above chance at
telling a compliant draft from a non-compliant one. This sharpens the claim and also qualifies
our own headline: the $\mathrm{F}_1 \approx 0.11$ we report elsewhere is a figure for one task
distribution, and a reader should not read a higher $\mathrm{F}_1$ on an easier benchmark as a
better detector. It is the base-rate-independent statistics that carry the mechanism.

\paragraph{But detection quality is necessary, not sufficient.} If a poor detector were the \emph{only} obstacle, a better
one should monotonically raise the loop's held-out score. It does not
(Table~\ref{tab:curve}). Read the second row with the taxonomy of
\S\ref{sec:method} in hand: for $57\%$ of constraint instances the tool's readout is
verdict-equivalent given the task statement, so that F$_1$ is largely measurement substituting
for judgement. The intrinsic critique (F$_1\!=\!0.11$) yields seed
$0.765$; the tool-fed critique (F$_1\!=\!0.83$) yields $0.837$; but the
\emph{oracle} critique (F$_1\!=\!1.0$ by construction) yields only $0.748$ --- the
best detector gives the \emph{worst} of the three. Detection quality is therefore
necessary but not sufficient, which independently matches
\citet{decomposing2026selfcorrection}'s finding that a model's detection ability does not
predict its correction success. Two non-exclusive mechanisms explain the inversion,
both pointing past detection: (i) the \emph{fix} step is the true bottleneck ---
even told exactly which constraints fail, the worker cannot reliably repair an exact
word count in one refine round; and (ii) \emph{actionability} matters more than
correctness --- the tool-fed critique emits instructive fix prose where the oracle
emits terse verdicts. Runs 9--10 test (ii) directly (\S\ref{sec:results}) and confirm
it, which resolves the tool${>}$oracle inversion as an actionability artefact rather
than evidence that a perfect detector hurts; mechanism (i) survives alongside it,
since even maximally-actionable feedback saturates at $0.873$.

\subsection{Fix-side levers: repair budget and feedback actionability}
\label{sec:fixside}

\paragraph{A perfect detector does not lift the ceiling (run 7).} The oracle-critique
control --- where the in-loop gate is the true metric --- was pre-registered to test
whether the loop \emph{can} convert a perfect detector into higher performance
(expected seed $\ge 0.85$). It cannot: the oracle seed is $0.748$, level with the
intrinsic baseline ($0.765$) and \emph{below} the tool baseline ($0.837$). Detection
quality is therefore \textbf{not} the binding constraint. Even told exactly which
constraints fail, \texttt{gpt-4.1-mini} cannot reliably fix hard constraints (e.g.
an exact word count) within the bounded refine budget. This is the crux
(\S\ref{sec:analysis}).

\paragraph{More fix attempts help, but saturate (run 8).} Holding the detector perfect
(oracle) and raising the refine budget $1 \to 3$ lifts the seed $0.748 \to 0.813$: the
\emph{fix} step is a genuine lever. But it still falls short of the pre-registered $0.85$
bar and below the tool baseline ($0.837$) --- a good tool at one retry roughly equals the
oracle at three. The residual is the generator's raw capability: perfect, repeated feedback
cannot push \texttt{gpt-4.1-mini} past $\sim\!0.81$ on hard constraints. The gains a
self-refine loop can extract (better detection $+$ more attempts) are real but bounded, and
prompt-only optimization already captures them. This is consistent with contemporaneous
code-generation findings that the feedback signal and the number of repair attempts --- not
the loop's structure --- drive self-repair gains \citep{feedbackoverform2026,howmanytries2026}.

\paragraph{Feedback \emph{actionability} is a real fix-side lever --- but it is feedback
shape, not GEPA (runs 9--10).} Runs 7--8 leave the residual fix-step gap ambiguous: is
the worker unable to \emph{act} on good feedback (generator-limited), or is the feedback
itself insufficiently actionable (feedback-limited)? We separate them by holding detection
fixed at the perfect oracle ($R{=}3$) and varying only the repair text handed to the refine
step across three pre-registered levels: \emph{lo} (a bare ``revise'' verdict), \emph{mid}
(which constraints failed, with their targets --- identical to run 8), and \emph{hi} (mid
\emph{plus} a concrete \emph{measured} edit per failed constraint, e.g.\ ``the draft has 2
words but needs exactly 20: add 18''). The primary contrast is decisive: $\dd(\text{hi}-
\text{lo}) = 0.873 - 0.810 = +0.063$ on the seed held-out (arm A, no GEPA), clearing the
pre-registered $+0.05$ threshold --- the fix step is partly \textbf{feedback-limited}.
All three seeds are positive ($+0.090, +0.025, +0.075$) and an exact-resample $95\%$
interval $[+0.025, +0.090]$ excludes zero, so the effect survives at $n{=}3$ --- though a
sign test at $n{=}3$ cannot fall below $p=0.25$, so this rests on the magnitudes rather than
on replication across seeds, and the whole comparison is a ceiling result measured with the
true metric gating at inference (\S\ref{sec:defect}). Two
sharp qualifications follow. (i) The gain is entirely in the \emph{hi} step: lo $\approx$
mid ($0.810$ vs.\ $0.813$), so naming \emph{which} constraint failed adds nothing over a
bare verdict; only the concrete, measured edit moves the needle. (ii) The lever is the
feedback's \emph{shape}, a hand-built measurement function --- \emph{not} loop-level GEPA:
$\dd(D-B)=0.00$ at every level, and optimization again slightly \emph{degrades} both arms
(hi seed $0.873 \to$ opt $0.86$). Finally, \emph{hi} ($0.873$) is the first oracle
configuration to \emph{exceed} the tool baseline ($0.837$), resolving the run-4 tool
$>$ oracle inversion (\S\ref{sec:detector}) as an actionability effect: the oracle
underperformed the tool at $R{=}1$ because its verdicts were terse, not because a perfect
detector hurts. Yet even maximal, exact repair guidance saturates at $0.873$ (still short of
perfect, no ceiling flag) --- so a generator-limited residual remains. The fix step is thus
\emph{both} feedback-limited (up to $\sim\!+0.06$ from actionability) and, beyond that,
generator-limited.

\paragraph{External feedback lifts the baseline (run 4).} Adding the measurement tool to the
critique step raised the seed loop from $0.765$ (run 3) to $0.837$, a direct confirmation of
the external-feedback prediction \citep{huang2023cannot}: the loop pays when the signal comes
from outside the model, and \S\ref{sec:analysis} shows why. Optimization then \emph{degraded}
both arms (mean $-0.04$), an early instance of the effect \S\ref{sec:rerun} measures cleanly.
A third \texttt{plan} module (run 5) moved nothing on any arm, so more optimizable structure
did not help either.


\subsection{The residual is one constraint, not a diffuse ceiling}
\label{sec:residual}

We decompose
the fix-step residual by constraint: replaying each run's cassette (offline, \$0;
\texttt{scripts/\allowbreak analyze\_residual\_\allowbreak by\_constraint.py}), we take the loop's \emph{final}
draft for every held-out task and record the per-constraint satisfaction rate
(Table~\ref{tab:residual}). The result is stark. Under the oracle detector with
maximally-actionable feedback (run 10), every ``choice'' constraint --- \texttt{no\_comma},
\texttt{all\_lowercase}, \texttt{num\_bullets}, \texttt{include\_keyword},
\texttt{end\_with}, \texttt{forbidden\_word} --- is satisfied at rate $1.0$, and
\texttt{word\_max\_length} at $0.90$. The residual is almost entirely
\texttt{exact\_word\_count} (with a small remainder in \texttt{word\_max\_length}):
even told exactly ``the draft has 2 words but needs exactly 20: add 18,''
\texttt{gpt-4.1-mini} hits the exact count only $\sim\!0.50$ of the time (up from
$0.16$ intrinsic, $0.33$ with which-constraint feedback). This is the known
inability of LLMs to control exact token counts during generation. So the
generator-limited residual is not a broad capability wall --- it is a single mechanical
skill, of a kind a deterministic actuator (not prompt/critique optimization) would

\subsection{Cross-domain corroboration: BigCodeBench-Hard}
\label{sec:bcb}

To test whether the instruction-following null generalizes to a domain where the
verify step catches real \emph{semantic} errors and runs as an executable tool ---
the regime \citet{shinn2023reflexion} find self-correction works --- we replicated
the four-arm design on \textbf{BigCodeBench-Hard} \citep{zhuo2024bigcodebench}, hard
enough that strong models score far below their full-benchmark pass@1 on it (the Hard
subset is calibrated to stay challenging). An earlier attempt on MBPP+
\citep{liu2023evalplus} saturated its training split, and that failure is what motivated
the move to a frontier-hard benchmark.\footnote{The MBPP+ attempt used the base tests as
the in-loop verifier and the edge-case tests as the hidden scorer. Every arm returned the
seed genome's held-out score on both seeds, so $\dd(D{-}B)=0$ identically --- a degenerate
artifact rather than a null: GEPA logged \texttt{``All subsample scores perfect for parent
0. Skipping''} on every iteration, because the seed loop, aided by executable base-test
feedback, already solved all six training tasks. Held-out headroom looked adequate
($0.80$--$0.90$); the \emph{training} split had none, and that is what gates GEPA. We did
not weaken the worker to manufacture headroom, because prompt-optimization and
self-refinement gains scale inversely with model strength
\citep{zhou2025bbopromptscale,zhang2024smallverifiers}, so a positive $\dd(D{-}B)$ bought
that way would attenuate toward zero at frontier scale; moving headroom into the
\emph{tasks} keeps the worker in the same capability-relative regime a frontier model
occupies on frontier-hard work. The run reproduces bit-for-bit and its cassette is released
with the others.} Each task's \texttt{unittest} methods are partitioned into a visible half
(the in-loop, executable signal) and a disjoint hidden half (the held-out scorer),
reconstructing the anti-oracle split; solutions are graded in a pinned, offline Docker
sandbox (\texttt{-{}-network none}, memory/PID caps, single-thread BLAS).\footnote{Executing
untrusted \emph{model} code at scale surfaces a subtlety absent when only reference solutions
run: a draft can exhaust the container's memory cap. Because we run one capped container at a
time on a host with ample free RAM, such an out-of-memory kill is the \emph{solution's} failure
(scored $0.0$), not a host fault --- distinct from a genuine infrastructure fault (daemon down,
image/mount error), which aborts loudly rather than silently recording a $0.0$.} We ran arms
A--D on $2$ seeds, $8$ train / $10$ held-out tasks drawn from a sandbox-verified
$80$-task pool, worker \texttt{gpt-4.1-mini}, reflection \texttt{gpt-5.6-terra}, at
$\mathrm{mmc}=30$.

The seed baseline sits at $0.40$ (seed 0) and $0.50$ (seed 1) --- far from both floor
and ceiling, so the loop has genuine headroom, unlike MBPP+. And unlike MBPP+, GEPA
\emph{ran}: on seed 1 it accepted improving candidates for \emph{both} the prompt-only
arm B (evolving \texttt{generate}) and the loop-level arm D (evolving \texttt{generate}
\emph{and} \texttt{ensure\_correct}), each lifting the $8$-task training aggregate
$0.375 \to 0.625$; on seed 0 neither arm accepted a candidate within budget, and the
genome-blind control C is flat by construction. Yet on the held-out split \emph{every
arm scored exactly its seed baseline} ($0.40$, $0.50$): the accepted training
improvements changed which held-out tasks \texttt{gpt-4.1-mini} solves not at all. The
headline is therefore a clean $\dd(D-B)=0.00$ (exact-resample $95\%$ CI
$[0.000, 0.000]$) --- and, more pointedly, even when loop-level optimization has its
extra lever and \emph{pulls} it (an accepted \texttt{ensure\_correct} rewrite that
improves training), it yields no held-out gain over prompt-only. The
instruction-following null recurs in a semantically-verified, executable-feedback
domain: train-split improvement does not transfer, and the loop-level lever is inert
on held-out.

\paragraph{Actionable feedback lifts the code baseline too (cross-domain D2$'$).} The one
intervention that helped on instruction-following --- concrete, measurement-shaped repair
feedback (runs 9--10) --- transfers to code, and \emph{more strongly}. Holding the arm at A
(no GEPA, so the lever is feedback \emph{shape}, not optimization) and varying only the in-loop
visible-test feedback between a bare verdict (\emph{lo}) and the verdict plus the source of the
visible tests the solution must satisfy (\emph{hi}), the held-out (hidden-test) score rises by
a mean $+0.20$ across six seeds (per-seed $+0.40, 0.00, +0.20, +0.20, +0.20, +0.20$). An
exact-resample $95\%$ interval is $[+0.100, +0.300]$, \emph{excluding zero} --- a
$3\times$-larger effect than the instruction-following actionability lift ($+0.063$), plausibly
because a code assertion is a sharper repair target than a word-count. Two qualifications hold
as on instruction-following: the gain accrues to the \emph{baseline}, not to $\dd(D-B)$ (which
stays $0.00$), so it is feedback \emph{shape}, not loop-level optimization --- GEPA did not need
to discover it; and the held-out scores carry some execution non-determinism (the effect size
dwarfs it, but we do not over-read the exact value). This reinforces the paper's conclusion:
the productive lever is the fixer and the actionability of its feedback, not loop-level
optimization of the critique.

\paragraph{Can GEPA \emph{discover} the actionable feedback? The actuator experiment.}
The $+0.20$ lift establishes that the fixer's feedback \emph{matters} --- but that feedback
was hand-designed. The sharpest test of our thesis is whether GEPA can \emph{discover} the
gain by optimization when pointed squarely at it. We add a fourth evolvable gene, an
\texttt{actuator}: an LLM step that, on a failed critique, turns the visible-test signal and
the visible-test source (the same \emph{hi} material) into the repair guidance fed to the
refine step. Arm~B still evolves only \texttt{generate}; arm~D now co-evolves \texttt{generate}
\emph{and} \texttt{actuator} with the detector (\texttt{ensure\_correct}) held \emph{fixed}, so
any $D-B$ gap isolates optimization of the fixer's feedback --- the one component we know can
help. A first run ($2$ seeds, $8$/$10$) gave a flat $\dd(D-B)=0.00$: GEPA proposed sensible
actuator rewrites (e.g.\ ``produce concrete, draft-specific repair instructions; do not write
vague advice such as `fix the syntax' ''), but accepted none that beat even the training
subsample, and the single accepted \texttt{generate} rewrite did not transfer.

A \emph{powered} replication ($3$ seeds, $20$ train / $20$ held-out, $\mathrm{mmc}=50$)
reproduced the null. Prompt-only arm~B now transfers to held-out (mean $+0.067$), while arm~D
does worse: $\dd(D-B) = -0.10$ (per-seed $-0.15, -0.15, 0.00$; exact-resample $95\%$ CI
$[-0.150, +0.000]$, which \emph{includes} zero; sign $p = 0.50$). \textbf{Two corrections
apply to this paragraph as first written} (\S\ref{sec:defect}): we reported the interval as
$[-0.150,-0.050]$, ``excluding zero'', which was a percentile-index error; and we explained
the deficit as arm~D over-fitting a larger search space, which the harness contradicts, since
under the default one-component-per-proposal selector arm~D searched the shared gene roughly
\emph{half} as hard as arm~B. What remains sayable is the ordering --- across all three code
configurations $D \le B$, never $D > B$ --- and that $-0.10$ on $20$ binary-scored tasks is
two task flips against a seed spread of four to five. Raising train size, resolution, seeds
and search depth surfaced no hidden positive for an arm whose extra gene the harness was not
equipped to optimize.

\paragraph{Reproducibility scope.} The main four-arm result reproduced identically across two
independent paid runs, and we release cassettes and scores. We do \emph{not} claim
byte-for-byte replay of the full call trace here: model solutions may use unseeded randomness,
so re-executing them can yield varying test output --- an intrinsic property of
executable-code benchmarks, not of our harness. (The instruction-following runs, which never
execute model code, do replay bit-for-bit.) We normalize the string-level non-determinism we
can: object ids, addresses, and temp paths in failure messages. The actuator runs share this
scope.


\subsection{The \texorpdfstring{$p_0$}{p0} sweep: when loop-level optimization pays}
\label{sec:p0}

Every result above was measured at one point on one axis. \S\ref{sec:analysis} explains the
loss with a mechanism that makes a quantitative commitment, and that commitment turns out to be
the difference between a null and a law. Write $p_0$ for the base rate of first drafts that
already satisfy every constraint. The training-signal \emph{cost} of an always-\texttt{FIX}
critique is the chance it damages a draft that needed no repair, which is proportional to
$p_0$. So the degenerate candidate is cheap when $p_0$ is small and expensive when it is large,
and the mechanism predicts a \textbf{crossing} rather than a constant:

\begin{quote}
Reflective optimization of a verifier gene converges on a non-accepting detector when $p_0$ is
low enough that unconditional refinement costs less on the training signal than discriminating
correctly; as $p_0$ rises, the same search must learn to emit \texttt{PASS}, and the loop-level
arm should stop losing.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{The lever, and why it was already in the data.}
\texttt{exact\_word\_count} is satisfied on only $0.16$ of held-out final drafts
(Table~\ref{tab:residual}) and the generator puts it in \emph{every} task of the hard tier, so
full-task success cannot exceed $0.16$ there: the tier's $p_0$ is pinned low by one mechanical
skill. Excluding it from the generated pool raises $p_0$ while leaving a brutal anchor in every
task. $p_0$ itself needs no dedicated measurement run --- the first critique event judged the
first draft, so any recorded cassette contains it
(\texttt{scripts/measure\_p0.py}) --- and one \$0.09 arm-A pass creates the cassette per level.
Three levels, arms~B and~D, three seeds, everything else held at \S\ref{sec:rerun}'s
configuration, \$1.79 per optimizing level.

\paragraph{The registered predictions, and the one that failed.} We registered three before
paying. (i) \texttt{PASS} rate $0.00$ at $L_1$: \textbf{held}, on three of three seeds.
(ii) \texttt{PASS} rate $>0$ on at least two of three seeds at $L_3$: \textbf{held}, on exactly
two. (iii) $\dd(D-B)$ non-decreasing in $p_0$ across $L_1 < L_2 < L_3$: \textbf{falsified} ---
$L_3$'s $-0.002$ sits below $L_2$'s $+0.060$.

We report (iii) as failed rather than reinterpreted, and the reason is a design error of ours
that the reader should be able to hold against the result. At $L_3$ the seed genome already
scores $0.942$, leaving $0.058$ of headroom, and both arms consume nearly all of it ($0.972$ and
$0.970$). Excluding \texttt{word\_max\_length} on top of \texttt{exact\_word\_count} recreated
precisely the condition this paper already dismisses for the easy tier --- saturation, where no
contrast between arms can appear because there is no room for one. $L_3$ therefore cannot
discriminate the arms on the outcome measure, although its \emph{mechanism} measure still moved
as predicted. The informative comparison is $L_1$ against $L_2$, and a corrected high-$p_0$
level needs a difficulty knob that raises $p_0$ without raising the baseline --- which the
constraint-exclusion lever, being a difficulty knob, cannot do.

\paragraph{The mechanism moved, and in a sharper way than predicted.} Scoring each arm's

Three things follow. First, prediction (ii): arm~D's evolved critique stops refusing to accept
as $p_0$ rises, on $0/3$, $1/3$ and $2/3$ seeds. Second, and unpredicted, \textbf{it never
emits a false \texttt{PASS} at any level} --- pooled $\mathrm{fp} = 0$ throughout, against the
seed rubric's $6$, $6$ and $2$. So the search does not trade accuracy for acceptance; it
acquires \emph{precision-1} acceptance, accepting only where it is right and continuing to
refine everything else. Third, the comparator forces a correction to how we first stated this.
The evolved critique is \emph{more} conservative than the seed rubric at every level, not less:
at $L_2$ it emits four \texttt{PASS}es where the seed rubric emits nineteen, and it still wins
by $+0.060$. So the $L_2$ gain is not ``the loop-level arm learned to accept''. It is that
arm~D's critique buys its acceptances at precision~1 while arm~B's are $6/19$ wrong, and a
false \texttt{PASS} ends the loop on a draft that still needed repair. What changes with $p_0$
is how much that discrimination is worth.

\paragraph{What this does to the headline.} Loop-level optimization is not inert. It is
\emph{conditionally} harmful, and the condition is the one the mechanism named. Every
instruction-following run in this paper, superseded or not, sits at the low-$p_0$ end of the
axis --- the hard tier mandates \texttt{exact\_word\_count}, which caps $p_0$ at $0.16$
(\S\ref{sec:residual}) --- and where we measure it directly it is $0.067$: the end of the axis
where refusing to accept is nearly free. At $p_0 = 0.267$ the same harness, optimizer, budget
and seeds give $\dd(D-B) = +0.060$ with every seed agreeing. One caveat belongs with that
number: at $n=3$ with three same-signed deltas the exact-resample interval \emph{must} exclude
zero, so ``excludes zero'' here is equivalent to sign-unanimity and the sign test is at its
floor ($p = 0.25$). It is suggestive, not significant, and we say so because this paper already
had to retract one interval-excludes-zero claim (Appendix~\ref{sec:defect}, defect~4). The
load-bearing evidence is not the interval but the \emph{registered} sign flip. The saturation
bias runs the safe way at $L_2$ as well: arm~D is nearer the ceiling ($0.960$, $0.975$, exactly
$1.000$), so a ceiling compresses its advantage rather than manufacturing it.

\subsection{How much of \texorpdfstring{$\dd$}{Delta} is the treatment?}
\label{sec:decomp}

Every $\dd(D-B)$ above is a \emph{whole-system} contrast, and the arms do not differ in one
component. Arm~B evolves \texttt{generate}; arm~D evolves \texttt{generate} \textbf{and}
\texttt{ensure\_correct}. Only the second is the treatment, so a gap between the arms mixes a
differently-evolved shared gene with the loop built on top of it, and $\dd$ alone cannot say
which moved. Writing $d$ for an arm's mean first-draft score and $f$ for its mean final score,
the split is an identity:
\[
\dd \;=\; f_D - f_B \;=\; \underbrace{(d_D - d_B)}_{\text{draft term: }\texttt{generate}}
\;+\; \underbrace{(f_D - d_D) - (f_B - d_B)}_{\text{loop term: the treatment}}
\]
Both terms come from the released artifact at \$0 --- the drafts by replaying each arm's evolved
genome against its cassette, the finals from the per-task vectors every run now emits
(\texttt{scripts/analyze\_delta\_decomposition.py}). The pooled $\dd$ it reconstructs agrees
with the arm means the paid runs reported, which is what makes it a decomposition of the
headline rather than of a different quantity.

component alone: the loop term runs $-0.017 \to +0.060 \to -0.007$. We report the aggregate

that answers the paper's title question, and a componentized-optimization result should print
both. This is the same lesson as the reporting minimum of Appendix~\ref{sec:defect}, one level
up: knowing that the optimizer \emph{changed} a component does not tell you that the component
is what moved the score.

\paragraph{Where the loss sits, and the mechanism it does not support.} The always-\texttt{FIX}
account says the cost of never accepting is borne by drafts that needed no repair, a stratum
whose weight is $p_0$. That is directly checkable. Partition held-out tasks by whether the
\emph{seed} genome's first draft was already correct --- a pre-treatment covariate, identical

At $L_1$ the already-correct stratum contributes $+0.002$: arm~D scores \emph{better} there

$28$ drafts that were already wrong. The stratum the mechanism names is also, at $p_0 = 0.067$,
structurally too thin to carry the result --- its weight is $2/30$, so with per-task scores in
$[0,1]$ its contribution is bounded by $0.067$ in magnitude whatever happens inside it. We
therefore \textbf{withdraw the outcome-side half of the always-\texttt{FIX} account}: the
training-signal argument for why a search converges on never-accepting stands and is what
predicted the sign, but ``it loses by damaging correct drafts'' is not what the $L_1$ loss
consists of. At $n=2$ this rules the channel out as the dominant term rather than estimating
it, and that limitation is the finding: a mechanism whose stratum has two tasks in it cannot be
confirmed at low $p_0$, only bounded.

What the loop term at $L_1$ does consist of is visible in the refine rounds. Arm~D refines
$30/30$ held-out tasks against arm~B's $24/30$ --- its critique never accepts, arm~B's falsely
accepts six --- yet each of arm~D's refine rounds is worth less, $+0.043$ against $+0.075$. The
refine step is net \emph{positive} at every level on the seed genome ($+0.072$, $+0.160$,
$+0.042$), so this is not a destructive repair step being invoked too often; it is that the
${\sim}3000$-character evolved rubric produces less productive repair prose than the
$127$-character seed rubric. That connects the $p_0$ result to this paper's strongest
established lever, feedback \emph{actionability} (\S\ref{sec:fixside}, worth $+0.063$): the
evolved gene moved the threshold to zero and made the feedback worse, and at $L_1$ the second
effect dominates. One caveat is load-bearing and cuts against reading the per-refine gap as an
estimate: arm~B refines a \emph{selected} subset, having skipped the six drafts its critique
waved through, so part of that gap is selection rather than feedback quality.


\section{Why the Lever Is Inert at Low \texorpdfstring{$p_0$}{p0}}
\label{sec:analysis}

\paragraph{What the optimizer actually wrote, scored as a detector.}
The re-run makes answerable a question a hand-written gene could only half-address. Below we
show that a verify gene written \emph{by hand} against our own findings moved only the
detector's threshold and never gave it discrimination; the obvious objection was that a
search over prose might find what one author could not. Arm~D's evolved genes are real
rubrics of ${\sim}3000$ characters, so we scored each one as a detector on exactly the drafts
it judged, against the seed rubric on the drafts \emph{it} judged (offline, \$0;
\texttt{scripts/analyze\_evolved\_gene.py}). A same-drafts contrast does not exist, because
the generator and critique genes moved together, but the question here is whether the evolved
detector discriminates at all.

\textbf{The evolved critique emits \texttt{PASS} zero times, on every seed.} Pooled, the
$127$-character seed rubric produces one true positive and the ${\sim}3000$-character evolved
rubrics produce none. Given a large budget of prose and a reflector that could finally see
its own verdicts, the optimizer converged on the same degenerate corner the hand-written gene
slid toward: never accept. This is the strongest form of the C1 finding, because it now holds
for the \emph{search} and not merely for one author. A critique that never passes sends every
draft to a refine round, including drafts that were already correct: on seed~2 two compliant
drafts were rejected. The loop-level arm does not lose because the optimizer failed at its
job; it loses because it succeeded at optimizing a training signal that rewards refusing to
accept.

One step of that account does not survive measurement, and \S\ref{sec:decomp} is where we
check it. The natural next sentence --- that refining an already-correct draft breaks it, and
that this is what the $-0.042$ consists of --- is wrong on both counts here. Those two
compliant drafts are the entire already-correct stratum at $L_1$, and arm~D scores $0.8000$ on
them against prompt-only's $0.7750$: the refine round did not break them. The deficit sits on
the drafts that were already \emph{wrong}. The training-signal argument for \emph{why the
search goes here} stands; the outcome-side argument for \emph{what it costs} needs the
decomposition, which also finds that $-0.025$ of the $-0.042$ is not this gene at all.

Why that corner is so attractive is worth stating, because it is not specific to our setup.
On multi-hard almost every first draft genuinely fails, so a detector that emits \texttt{FIX}
unconditionally is nearly indistinguishable, \emph{on the training signal}, from one that
reasons correctly --- and it is far easier to express in prose. The penalty appears only on
the minority of drafts that were already compliant, which a small training split barely
contains. That is Goodhart's law operating inside the loop: the component was optimized
against a proxy on which the degenerate policy is almost free, and the cost lands on
held-out. Any componentized optimizer pointed at a verifier on a task with a low base rate of
correct first drafts faces the same gradient, which makes ``never accept'' a failure mode to
test for rather than a quirk of this run --- and it is cheap to test for, since it shows up
as a \texttt{PASS} rate of zero.

\paragraph{The same corner is reachable by hand.} The result above is the search finding the degenerate corner. We had already found it by hand, before buying the re-run, and the two conditions are the first runs recorded under the corrected harness.

\medskip\noindent
Every mechanism above is measured on \emph{observed} critiques. It leaves open the
question that decides whether the loop-level lever is worth optimizing at all: is there a
better verify gene to be found? An optimizer here may only rewrite prose, so the ceiling
on what it could possibly discover is what prose can do, and that is cheap to bound
directly. We wrote a verify gene by hand against this paper's own findings --- it forces an
explicit measurement before each verdict, addresses false passes and false alarms
separately, and demands measured-value / target-value / smallest-edit repair text rather
than a restatement of the rule --- and ran arm~A twice on multi-hard, seed~0, $32$ held-out
tasks, changing \emph{only} the \texttt{ensure\_correct} gene. The two conditions share one
content-addressed cassette, so each task's first draft is byte-identical across them and
each pair isolates the gene exactly: same draft in, different critique, different repair.
The analysis was pre-registered and committed before the run was paid for
(\texttt{scripts/analyze\_c1.py}); total cost \$0.043.

Paired by task, the hand-written gene does not help: $n{=}32$, $17$ ties, better on $6$
tasks and worse on $9$, mean $\dd = -0.033$ with a bootstrap $95\%$ interval of
$[-0.095,+0.028]$ and sign test $p=0.61$. Our pre-registered rule returns
\textbf{inconclusive}, and deliberately not ``no effect'': the $90\%$ interval extends past
the $\pm0.05$ SESOI, so equivalence fails too. On its own that is an underpowered null.

What the same cassette shows about the \emph{detector} is sharper (Table~\ref{tab:c1}). The
hand-written gene did change its behaviour, and markedly: it emitted PASS zero times
against three, sending $32/32$ tasks to a refine round against $29/32$. But the change is
confined to one axis. Neither gene produced a single true positive on the five compliant
drafts in this split, exactly as the intrinsic critique of run~3 managed one true positive
out of five while emitting twelve false ones. Both sit at the chance diagonal; the
hand-written gene slid \emph{along} it, toward predicting negative always. What the
threshold move itself bought is small and legible: on the three tasks whose false pass it
correctly caught, the score rose $0.800 \to 0.867$ (one repaired to $1.0$, two unchanged),
worth $+0.006$ on the 32-task mean; on the five tasks whose draft was already compliant the
mean is $-0.100$, and since \emph{both} genes demanded fixes on all five, that is one damaged
draft rather than a false-alarm difference. Those eight tasks are the only ones on which the
two genes' \emph{verdicts} differ, and together they account for $-0.009$ of the $-0.033$: the
remaining $-0.023$ falls on the twenty-four tasks where both genes demanded a fix and only the
repair \emph{prose} differed. So eliminating false passes bought a real gain an order of
magnitude below the noise it sits in, and the larger part of the movement is not the threshold
at all --- it is the rewritten repair text, which is the lever \S\ref{sec:fixside} measures
directly.

This is a mechanism for the loop-level null that does not involve the optimizer at all. If
rewriting this gene's prose moves where its threshold sits without giving it discriminative
power, then a search over prose searches a space whose reachable improvements are threshold
moves, and a threshold move in a detector that cannot discriminate has no systematic gain to
offer. It is evidence, not proof: one hand-written point in prose space does not establish
that no prose adds discrimination, and a competent author might do better. It does mean a
loop-level null should no longer be read primarily as a fact about optimizers. This is what
made the corrected-harness re-run worth buying and what it was pre-registered to predict
(\S\ref{sec:directions}); \S\ref{sec:rerun} reports the outcome.

\paragraph{Intrinsic self-correction has a low ceiling, and optimizing on top of it
costs.} Consistent with \citet{huang2023cannot}, run~4 shows the loop improving when given an
external tool but the reflective optimization on top adding noise rather than signal --- the
``self-correction can make it worse'' regime --- and its optimized held-out scores fall below
the seed, echoing OPRO's observation that optimized instructions can overfit a training set
\citep{yang2023opro}. With small budgets and small splits, proposals that win on the training
subsample lose on held-out, and this hurts B and D alike. \S\ref{sec:rerun} measures the
effect on a harness where the two arms actually differ.


\section{A Positive Control: the Instrument Does Respond}
\label{sec:posctrl}

\paragraph{A positive control: the instrument does respond.}
A null is worth reading only if the instrument that produced it can register a signal, and
after three silent failures we do not ask the reader to take that on trust. We built a
landscape in which evolving the verify gene is the \emph{only} path to a passing answer: the
generator always drafts \texttt{x\,x}, which fails the task's single constraint however its
prompt is worded; the critique catches the flaw only when the verify gene carries a sentinel;
and a caught flaw triggers a refine that passes. Arm~B therefore cannot escape the failing
draft no matter how well it optimizes, and arm~D can. Running the real four-arm study through
real GEPA on this landscape (no stubbed optimizer, no network, no spend) gives
A~$0.000$, B~$0.000$, C~$0.000$, D~$\mathbf{1.000}$, i.e.\ $\dd(D-B) = +1.000$. The harness
detects a loop-level advantage when one exists, so the near-zero $\dd$ we report on real tasks
is a property of those tasks and that optimizer, not of the measurement path
(\texttt{scripts/positive\_control.py}).


\section{Pre-registered: Is the Loss a Small-Split Artifact?}
\label{sec:prereg}
\label{sec:splitsweep}

Two mechanisms now account for arm~D's $-0.042$ at $L_1$: the training signal that rewards
refusing to accept (\S\ref{sec:analysis}), and the \texttt{generate} gene that carries $-0.025$
of it (\S\ref{sec:decomp}). One alternative explanation survives both, and the $p_0$ result
does not touch it: eight training tasks is a small split, arm~D has two genes of surface to
overfit rather than one, and the reflective optimizer overfits. That is testable by varying the
split, the harness already exposes \texttt{-{}-n-train}, and we pre-register the design here,
before paying, in the same form as the prediction that Section~\ref{sec:rerun} resolved.

\paragraph{A fixed-budget split sweep is not a split sweep.} The obvious experiment ---
raise \texttt{-{}-n-train} and hold \texttt{-{}-max-metric-calls} fixed --- is confounded, and
in the direction that manufactures the result we are looking for. GEPA charges its seed
evaluation $|\mathrm{valset}|$ metric calls, and our \texttt{optimize\_loop} passes the train
split as the trainset with no separate valset, so the valset \emph{is} the train split. One
accept cycle therefore costs $n_{\mathrm{train}}$ (seed evaluation) $+\,\min(3,
n_{\mathrm{train}})$ (reflection minibatch) $+\,n_{\mathrm{train}}$ (the full re-evaluation
that promotes a proposal). At $n_{\mathrm{train}}{=}24$ that is $51$ calls; run~3's budget of
$28$ would have bought an arm that cannot complete a single cycle, would have reported
\texttt{genes\_changed=[]} and a flat $\dd$, and would have read as \emph{optimization did not
help} when it meant \emph{optimization never ran}. This is Evidence~3's confound
(\S\ref{sec:defect}) in a new place, and the honest thing to note is that we caught it this
time before the money rather than after the write-up. The harness now refuses the
configuration at the dry-run step with the arithmetic in the error text, and the refusal is
scoped to arms that actually evolve something, so the two \texttt{-{}-arms A} verifier-ablation
runs (which optimize nothing, and where the budget is inert) still stand.

The sweep therefore holds \emph{metric calls per training task} constant at $3.5$ --- a
constant number of epochs over the split --- which reproduces run~3's budget exactly at
$n_{\mathrm{train}}{=}8$ and scales it elsewhere. The cost of that choice is stated plainly:
split size and total budget move together, so the sweep identifies their joint effect along
the constant-epoch line and not either one alone. They cannot be separated below the
accept-cycle floor, because the cell that would separate them is the degenerate one the guard
rejects; a budget-only sweep at fixed $n_{\mathrm{train}}{=}8$ (D4) is the complement.

\paragraph{What is held fixed, verified offline at \$0.} The pool is
$\max(64,\,2\max(n_{\mathrm{train}}, n_{\mathrm{heldout}}) + 2)$, hence $64$ for every
$n_{\mathrm{train}} \le 31$ and larger from $32$. Because the pool size seeds the shuffle,
crossing that cliff silently changes \emph{which} tasks are held out, so the sweep stays below
it and a test pins the cliff. Within it we verify, for all three seeds: the ten held-out tasks
are identical at every sweep size; the training splits are nested prefixes, so the sweep adds
tasks rather than resampling them; train and held-out share no task id and no exact instance;
and the type-overlap shares of Table~\ref{tab:splitsweep}. These are properties of the
generator, not of a purchase, so they are asserted in the test suite rather than reported once.

\paragraph{The prediction.} The mechanism of \S\ref{sec:analysis} predicts that a larger split
will \emph{not} rescue arm~D. What the search converges on is a critique that never returns
\texttt{PASS}, and unconditional refinement is near-free on the training signal whenever most
first drafts fail --- a property of the task's pass rate, not of the split size. So the
degenerate candidate stays optimal on train at every $n_{\mathrm{train}}$ we can afford, and
we predict (i) the evolved critique's \texttt{PASS} rate remains $0.00$ on at least five of the
six arm-D genes at $n_{\mathrm{train}} \in \{16, 24\}$, and (ii) $\dd(D-B)$ at
$n_{\mathrm{train}}{=}24$ does not cross zero. Prediction (i) is the load-bearing one: it is a
near-deterministic property of a released gene, checkable offline for \$0 by
\texttt{scripts/analyze\_evolved\_gene.py} --- the script that produced the detector counts
earlier in this section --- whereas (ii) is a three-seed mean whose interval will
again be wide enough that a sign flip alone would settle nothing. The honest alternative is
live and would be the more useful paper: if \texttt{PASS} becomes non-zero and $\dd$ closes on
zero as the split grows, then the finding is not ``loop-level optimization is inert'' but
``loop-level optimization needs a training split larger than any we bought,'' and the headline
should change accordingly.

\subsection{Result: the load-bearing prediction holds and the headline does not replicate}
\label{sec:splitresult}

The three unbought rows were then bought, under the priced bound of \$6.98 stated above, and
Table~\ref{tab:splitresults} reports them beside run~3. The harness records the pre-flight
\emph{estimate} for each run and not the invoice, so what is verifiable in the artifact is the
bound ($\$1.47 + \$2.43 + \$3.08$) rather than the spend; run~3's precedent puts the estimator
at roughly $6\times$ conservative. The two predictions come apart, so we take them in the order
of their pre-registered weight.

\textbf{Prediction (i) holds, at exactly its stated threshold.} The evolved critique's
\texttt{PASS} rate is $0.00$ on $3/3$ arm-D genes at $n_{\mathrm{train}}{=}16$ and $2/3$ at
$n_{\mathrm{train}}{=}24$: five of six, which is what we pre-registered as the load-bearing
claim. Pooling all four sizes it is $11/12$. The single exception is instructive rather than
awkward: that gene emits exactly one \texttt{PASS}, and the draft it accepted was in fact
correct ($\mathrm{tp}{=}1$, $\mathrm{fp}{=}0$). Across every arm-D gene in the sweep the
evolved critique never emits a false \texttt{PASS}, which is the same precision-1 acceptance
reported at the $p_0$ levels. So the Goodhart mechanism of \S\ref{sec:analysis} is not a
small-split artifact: at low $p_0$ the reflective search converges on a non-accepting verifier
at every training-split size we can afford.

\textbf{Prediction (ii) is falsified on the point estimate.} We predicted $\dd(D-B)$ at
$n_{\mathrm{train}}{=}24$ would not cross zero; it reads $+0.023$. Two of three seeds are still
negative and the interval $[-0.025,+0.115]$ contains zero, so nothing is established in the
other direction either --- but the prediction was registered as a sign claim about a point
estimate, and as a sign claim about a point estimate it failed. We report it that way rather
than retreating to the interval, which is the move pre-registration exists to prevent.

\textbf{What the sweep actually establishes is about run~3, not about arm~D.} Every row of
Table~\ref{tab:splitresults} holds $p_0$, the held-out tasks and the seeds fixed; only the
split and its constant-epoch budget move. Across them $\dd(D-B)$ reads
$+0.017,\,-0.042,\,+0.062,\,+0.023$: three of four positive, and the one negative cell is
run~3 itself. The sign of the effect this paper leads with is therefore not a property of
$p_0 = 0.067$; it is a property of one $(n_{\mathrm{train}}, \mathrm{mmc})$ cell. We withdraw
the unconditional form of that claim in \S\ref{sec:threats} and state what survives:
\emph{the verifier reliably degenerates at low $p_0$, and we have not shown that this costs
held-out performance.} The two are separable and we had run them together.

The refine accounting says why the two can come apart, and it is visible in released data at
\$0. A never-accepting critique is behaviourally just \emph{always refine}, and at low $p_0$
refining is worth doing: arm~D refines $30/30$ held-out drafts at both $n_{\mathrm{train}}{=}4$
and $16$ and gains $+0.055$ and $+0.098$ per refined draft. A degenerate verifier and a harmful
verifier are different objects, and where almost every draft is wrong the degenerate one is
close to the right policy.

\paragraph{The confound we priced, quantified after the fact.} Holding metric calls per
training task constant does not hold search depth constant. Promotable accept cycles,
$(\mathrm{mmc} - n_{\mathrm{train}})/(\min(3,n_{\mathrm{train}}) + n_{\mathrm{train}})$, rise
across the sweep as $1.43,\,1.82,\,2.11,\,2.22$, while the reflection minibatch falls from
$75\%$ of the split to $12\%$. Both move monotonically with $n_{\mathrm{train}}$ and neither
was held fixed, so a larger split also means a slightly deeper search over a less
representative minibatch. The starved arm~B at $n_{\mathrm{train}}{=}4$ is that confound
becoming visible: $1.43$ promotable cycles is enough budget to pass the guard and not enough
for a one-gene arm to reliably land an acceptance. This is the correct reading of the guard's
scope --- it enforces that an arm \emph{can} fund an accept cycle, never that one is accepted.

\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

We state these plainly. \textbf{Power:} runs 4--10 use $n{=}3$ seeds (runs 2--3 use 5), and
our earlier claim that the intervals were ``unusually informative despite small $n$'' is
withdrawn as circular (\S\ref{sec:defect}); we now report per-run tie counts alongside every
interval and an exact sign test. At $n{=}3$ that test cannot fall below $p=0.25$ even with
unanimous seeds, so the one positive effect, $\dd(\text{hi}-\text{lo})=+0.063$ with interval
$[+0.025,+0.090]$, rests on magnitudes rather than replication. \textbf{Measurement noise, and
what it costs the headline:} no interval in this study contains within-condition sampling
variance, because the worker runs at the provider's default temperature with one sample per
task, so nothing was measured twice on purpose. It was nonetheless measured by accident, and we
report it rather than leaving the omission as a scope note. Five released runs --- the four-arm
re-run and each row of the split sweep --- evaluate the \emph{same} unoptimized seed genome on
the \emph{same} thirty held-out tasks before optimizing anything, so they are five independent
draws of one fixed number. Their means are $0.7900$, $0.7883$, $0.7717$, $0.7583$, $0.7883$.
From the paired differences, $\sigma$ for one task under one draw is $0.108$; a $\dd$ compares
two independent draws, so $\sigma(\dd)$ on one task is $0.153$ and on a thirty-task mean
\[
\sigma(\dd) \;=\; 0.153/\sqrt{30} \;=\; 0.0279 .
\]
In those units the study's effects are: the $L_1$ loss $-0.042$ at $1.49\sigma$, the $L_2$ gain
$+0.060$ at $2.15\sigma$, the $L_3$ null $-0.002$ at $0.06\sigma$, and the actionability effect
$+0.063$ at $2.26\sigma$. \textbf{We therefore withdraw the unconditional claim that evolving
the verifier costs held-out performance at low $p_0$.} It rests on a single $1.49\sigma$ cell
that three other training-split sizes do not reproduce (\S\ref{sec:splitresult}). What survives
is narrower and better supported: the verifier reliably degenerates to non-acceptance at low
$p_0$ ($11/12$ genes across four split sizes), the $+0.060$ gain at higher $p_0$ is the
study's largest loop-level effect and is unanimous across seeds, and detector quality does not
predict loop performance. Two caveats on the estimate itself: the mean signed difference
between replicates, $-0.007$, is confounded with any provider-side drift between recording
dates, and five replicates of three seeds cannot separate the two. It establishes an order of
magnitude, and that order of magnitude is the same as the effects being reported. Direct
replicate sampling (D7) is the fix and needs no re-optimization.
\textbf{A do-nothing floor in the
metric:} the fraction-of-constraints-satisfied score is not anchored at zero for an empty
answer --- the empty string scores $0.548$ on the vendored IFBench slice and $0.420$ on
generated multi-hard, and five of eleven IFBench verifiers are vacuously satisfied by text
containing no whitespace, quotes or bullets (\texttt{\_sub\_bullets("")} reduces to
\texttt{all([]) = True}). These are faithful ports of the upstream checkers and the floor
applies to every arm equally, so it does not bias \dd, but it compresses the scale: on IFBench
the floor sits at or above our reported seed baselines. It also makes the metric a poor
instrument for this loop, since a metric that partly rewards saying less is exposed to a
critique that pushes toward shorter answers. That was open when first written, because no
released run carried its evolved gene text; the corrected harness releases all of them, so it
is now measured and did not happen. Mean final-answer length under the arm whose critique never
accepts is $27.3$ words against prompt-only's $26.4$ at $L_1$ ($42.3$ vs.\ $43.2$ at $L_2$,
$45.5$ vs.\ $46.1$ at $L_3$): the loop-level arm's answers are not systematically shorter, and
a refine round moves length by under two words at every level, against a floor that needs an
empty answer to reach. \textbf{Single worker/reflection
model:} results may be model-dependent \citep{huang2023cannot}. \textbf{Cross-lineage, not
cross-provider:} run 6's critic shares a vendor and training lineage with the worker, so it
breaks self-\emph{grading} but may retain self-preference bias (D3). \textbf{One task family:}
verifiable instruction-following may be a domain where the critique has little leverage.
\textbf{Budget:} $\mathrm{mmc}\in[20,64]$ is small. \textbf{Held-out generalization:} the
generator's parameter pools are small, so train and held-out tasks can share parameter
combinations; this affects all arms equally but softens the generalization claim.
Section~\ref{sec:directions} turns each threat into a concrete follow-up.

\paragraph{Which threats the released artifact can close.} The threats differ in what each
requires: more spend against the same harness, a credential we do not hold, or new harness
code. Table~\ref{tab:closability} separates them, so a reader can tell which open questions
are a budget line and which are engineering.

One replication predates the audit. A worker swap to \texttt{gpt-4.1-nano} on the multi-hard
regime ($3$ seeds, $16$/$16$, $\mathrm{mmc}=64$) gives $\dd(D-B) = -0.014$ (corrected interval
$[-0.041,+0.000]$; two of three seeds are ties), arm~B at $0.70 \pm 0.03$ and arm~D at
$0.69 \pm 0.03$ from a seed of $0.67$. It shows the pattern is not an artifact of one worker
model, but it inherits the same harness, so it is not a test of loop-level optimization. An
$\mathrm{mmc}{=}160$ replication was designed, priced, started and stopped mid-run once the
audit landed, having spent $\approx\!\$0.33$ of a projected $\$0.90$; its partial cassette is
not released, since a recording with no corresponding result would be a misleading artifact.

\section{When Would Loop-Level Help? Directions}
\label{sec:directions}

The null is not ``componentized optimization is useless''; it is ``the critique
component of a self-refine loop is not a productive optimization target when it
is an unreliable, self-referential detector on shallow-verification tasks.'' The
following directions test the boundary; each changes one variable.

\emph{Done in this paper:} D2 (critique-as-detector, \S\ref{sec:analysis}), a
cross-lineage critic (run 6), the oracle ceiling (run 7), a fix-budget sweep
(run 8), the feedback-actionability ablation (D2$'$, runs 9--10), the $p_0$ sweep
(\S\ref{sec:p0}) and the component decomposition of $\dd$ (\S\ref{sec:decomp}). What runs
7--10 changed: the priority is no longer ``get a better detector'' --- a perfect
one does not suffice --- but ``fix the fixer,'' and we now know the fixer is
\emph{partly} feedback-limited (actionability buys $\sim\!+0.06$) and \emph{partly}
generator-limited (the residual). The agenda, re-ordered accordingly:

\begin{description}
  \item[D1$'$ (\emph{fix}-side interventions).] The binding constraint is repair,
    not detection. Run 8 did the refine-budget half ($R{:}1\!\to\!3$, $+0.065$).
    \emph{Remaining:} a stronger worker on refinement only, and constraint-specific
    repair \emph{tools} (e.g.\ a deterministic word-count trimmer) that let the model
    \emph{act} on feedback --- the natural next step given the generator-limited
    residual runs 9--10 expose.
  \item[D2$'$ (feedback actionability) --- \textbf{done, runs 9--10} (\S\ref{sec:results}).]
    Open follow-up: does an LLM \emph{rewriting} the measured edits into fluent prose add
    anything over the raw measurement?
  \item[D3 (true cross-family critic).] Repeat run 6 with a cross-\emph{provider}
    critic (\texttt{gemini-2.5-flash-lite} or \texttt{claude-haiku-4-5}; pre-priced,
    needs a key) at $\ge 8$ seeds. This is the one blocked purely on a credential, and
    the only direction that separates an intrinsically unreliable detector from a
    self-preferring one.
  \item[D4 (budget \& worker sweeps) --- \emph{now the complement to a bought result}.]
    $\mathrm{mmc}\in\{20,40,80,160\}$ at fixed $n_{\mathrm{train}}{=}8$, and worker
    $\in$\{nano, mini, a 5-series\} to rule out under-budgeting and map
    model-dependence. The split sweep (\S\ref{sec:splitresult}) moved split size and budget
    together along a constant-epoch line, by necessity, so it cannot say which of the two
    produced the swing from $-0.042$ to $+0.062$. Holding the split fixed and varying only the
    budget is the experiment that separates them, and it is the one place where a positive
    finding would still be available: if $\dd$ rises with budget at fixed split, the story is
    under-search rather than instability.
  \item[D5 (cross-domain) --- \textbf{done} (\S\ref{sec:bcb}).] The null recurs in a
    semantically-verified, executable-feedback domain, so reliable, actionable verification
    is not by itself sufficient to make the loop-level lever pay off; aiming GEPA directly
    at the fixer's feedback did not rescue it either. Math (a numeric checker) remains a
    natural next replication.
  \item[D7 (statistics) --- \emph{reinstated, sharpened, and now the study's highest-value
    purchase}.] The near-zero paired variance we
    once read as precision was mostly ties, i.e.\ the treatment not being applied
    ($\S\ref{sec:defect}$), and no interval in the study contains within-condition sampling
    variance at all: the worker runs at the provider's default temperature, one sample per task.
    That component has since been estimated offline at $\sigma(\dd) = 0.028$ from five runs that
    re-score one fixed seed genome (\S\ref{sec:threats}), and it was immediately decisive: it
    is what moved the $-0.042$ from a finding to a $1.49\sigma$ fluctuation. Estimating a
    variance from replicates that were recorded on different dates confounds it with
    provider-side drift, though, and $\sigma$ is what every remaining marginal claim now turns
    on. Repeated samples are therefore load-bearing rather than optional.
    The harness now supports the pinned temperature, and a per-call sample salt, because both
    are cassette traps rather than parameters: a temperature baked into the completion factory
    never enters the content-addressed key, so two runs at different temperatures would serve
    each other's completions; and since record mode dedupes identical calls, $K$ repeated
    samples without a salt collapse to one entry and return byte-identical draws, which would
    report \emph{zero} sampling variance --- the most flattering artifact available to a study
    whose whole subject is flattering artifacts. What remains is the repeat loop itself, which
    needs no re-optimization: the released genomes can be re-scored offline.
    More seeds are load-bearing too, but only where a seed buys a fresh sample: Evidence~4b
    shows that on the vendored families a seed merely re-partitions one fixed pool, so adding
    seeds there raises apparent precision without adding independent information. The unit to
    pair on is the \emph{task}, which is why every run now releases its per-task score vector,
    and a pre-registered replication should state which unit its interval resamples.
  \item[D1$'$-tools (deterministic actuator).] Table~\ref{tab:residual} localizes the
    residual to \texttt{exact\_word\_count}; a deterministic count-fixer would likely
    recover most of it. Lower priority now that the decomposition has made the point
    (a counter fixes counting); its value is confirming the other constraints do not
    break when the count is enforced.
  \item[D8 (generalization).] Enlarge the generator's parameter space so
    train/held-out cannot near-duplicate, and report the train--held-out gap.
  \item[D9 (a high-$p_0$ level that does not saturate) --- \emph{new, and the open design
    problem}.] The falsified monotonicity prediction (\S\ref{sec:p0}) needs a knob that raises
    $p_0$ \emph{without} raising the baseline, and constraint exclusion cannot supply one: it
    is a difficulty knob, so it moves both together and $L_3$ saturates. Two candidates
    separate them. (i) Hold the constraint mix fixed and vary the \emph{drafting} budget --- a
    second draft-then-pick step raises the share of already-correct first drafts entering the
    critique without making any task easier to score. (ii) Keep the hard tier and \emph{sample}
    tasks to a target $p_0$, which is now free to measure per task from any cassette
    (\S\ref{sec:p0}), so the level can be constructed to specification rather than discovered.
    Under either, the already-correct stratum stops being two tasks wide, which is what
    \S\ref{sec:decomp} needs to estimate the damage channel instead of merely bounding it.
\end{description}

\paragraph{A pre-registered prediction for the corrected-harness re-run --- \textbf{resolved,
\S\ref{sec:rerun}}.} The prediction below was committed before the run was paid for and is
reproduced unchanged below. Prediction (i) held decisively: \texttt{ensure\_correct} changed
on \emph{all three} seeds of arm~D. Prediction (ii) held on the point estimate and only
barely --- $\dd(D-B) = -0.042$ against a $\pm0.05$ SESOI --- and the interval
$[-0.095,+0.050]$ extends well past it, so we do not read this as equivalence. What we did
not predict is that the two optimizing arms would separate: prompt-only came out exactly
flat while loop-level cost $0.042$, which looked at the time like a worse outcome for the
thesis than the indifference we expected. It no longer reads that way: the split sweep this
section also pre-registered puts three other configurations of the same comparison on the
positive side, and the noise estimate puts this one at $1.49\sigma$
(\S\ref{sec:splitresult}, \S\ref{sec:threats}). \emph{The pre-registration, as written:} Section~\ref{sec:analysis}(1d)
found that a hand-written verify gene changed the detector's threshold (PASS rate $0.09 \to
0.00$) without producing a true positive, and moved held-out by $-0.033$ ($p=0.61$). If that
generalizes to the search --- if the reachable improvements in prose space are threshold moves
--- then the corrected harness should produce a specific, unusual pattern: \textbf{the
treatment is applied and still does nothing}. Concretely we predict (i) \texttt{genes\_changed}
will now include \texttt{ensure\_correct} on at least one seed of arm~D, since the reflector can
finally see the detector's own verdicts and the selector no longer starves it; and (ii)
$\dd(D-B)$ will nonetheless remain within our $\pm0.05$ SESOI. Prediction (i) is the one that
matters procedurally: it is the check the released runs could not perform, and if it fails the
re-run is again not a test of loop-level optimization and must be reported as such rather than
as a null. Prediction (ii) is the substantive one, and the honest alternative is live --- an
optimizer explores a far larger region of prose space than one author, so it may find the
discrimination we could not write.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Three questions are easily conflated; our answers differ. \textbf{(i) Does the
self-refine loop help?} Only through \emph{external} signal --- a measurement tool
(seed $0.765\!\to\!0.837$) or more refine attempts ($0.748\!\to\!0.813$) --- never
through the model grading its own draft (the intrinsic critique is a near-random
detector, F$_1\!=\!0.11$). \textbf{(ii) Does GEPA lift this task?} No: its optimized
genomes gave no held-out gain and sometimes \emph{degraded} both arms (train-split
overfitting), at the budgets tested --- not a verdict on GEPA in general, which
reports gains elsewhere. \textbf{(iii) Does loop-level optimization beat prompt-only
(the thesis)?} \textbf{It depends on $p_0$, and that is the paper's answer.} At $p_0 = 0.067$ it
costs $0.042$; at $p_0 = 0.267$ it gains $0.060$ with all three seeds agreeing, under the same
harness, optimizer, budget and seeds (\S\ref{sec:p0}). The mechanism named the condition in
advance: unconditional refinement is nearly free on a training signal where $p_0$ is small and
costly where it is not, and the evolved gene's \texttt{PASS} rate duly moves from zero to
non-zero as $p_0$ rises --- while never emitting a false \texttt{PASS}, so what it gains is
precision-1 acceptance. Every instruction-following run we bought, superseded or not, sat at the
low-$p_0$ end of an axis we had not identified, and the corrected re-run measures it at $0.067$. \emph{The null was real and it was
local.} Three qualifications travel with that answer. At $n=3$ per level no interval is
significant --- the sign test cannot fall below $p=0.25$ --- so the load-bearing evidence is the
registered sign flip rather than any one interval. One of our three registered predictions
(monotonicity in $p_0$) \emph{failed}, because the highest level saturates. And $\dd$ is a
whole-system contrast: decomposed, only $40\%$ of the $-0.042$ is the treated component, while
all of the $+0.060$ is (\S\ref{sec:decomp}) --- so the sign flip is cleaner on the component
than on the headline, and the headline overstates what optimizing a verifier costs.

That measurement cost us every contrast run that preceded it: \textbf{fifteen} of them, across
instruction following, a cross-domain replication on frontier-hard code (\S\ref{sec:bcb}), an
actuator variant and a worker-model swap. Ten reported $\dd(D{-}B)$ of exactly zero and the
rest reported near-zero or, in two cases, a moderate negative --- and none of them tested the
hypothesis, because the critique gene never changed (Appendix~\ref{sec:defect}). A zero
replicated ten times, from an experiment that did not run, alongside non-zeros that measure a
selector confound through the gene the arms share rather than the treatment
(Appendix~\ref{sec:superseded}). The generalizable lesson is not about our wiring but about what a
componentized-optimization result must print: \textbf{per component, whether the optimizer's
output differs from the seed}, one integer, which would have ended this line of work fifteen
runs earlier. That failure mode is not ours alone ---
\citet{pappu2026multiagent} independently report GEPA returning the seed prompt unchanged on
two benchmarks --- and it has a cheap companion screen, since the degenerate verifier this
paper ends at announces itself as a \texttt{PASS} rate of zero.

What we can still say divides cleanly. \textbf{About the loop:} the intrinsic critique is a
near-random failure detector --- at chance on Youden's $J$ and Matthews' correlation across a
$10\times$ range of $p_0$, with the $\mathrm{F}_1 = 0.11$ we quote being a figure for one task
distribution rather than a property of the detector --- which quantifies the practitioner
intuition that a model grading its own homework is too lenient
\citep{osmani2026loop,kamoi2024whencan}; \emph{given} a perfect checker at inference, a
perfect detector still does not maximize held-out score, so detection is not the binding
constraint there either; the repair signal's \emph{shape} is worth $+0.063$ (text) and
$+0.20$ (code) while naming the failed constraint adds nothing; and the residual concentrates
in one mechanical skill, exact counting, that no prompt rewrite fixed. \textbf{About
componentized optimization:} one underpowered configuration in which the treatment demonstrably
ran and bought nothing, plus the observation that optimizing at all cost held-out score on
both arms. For fifteen runs the answer was ``nothing yet'', because the apparatus we built to
answer the question quietly answered a different one; the twelfth is a first data point, not
a verdict.

\textbf{About building such apparatus}, which is the contribution we did not plan. A
hill-climbing loop improves a harness by attributing trace evidence to components
\citep{langchain2026loopcraft}; if that attribution is wrong, the optimizer rewrites the wrong
thing and every downstream number stays plausible. Two defaults were enough to cause it here:
a reflective dataset shared across components, and a selector that rewrites one component per
proposal. Neither is exotic; one is a library default. The cheap defence is to report, per
component, whether the optimizer's output differs from the seed at all --- one integer per
gene, which we now emit and which would have caught this fifteen runs earlier. That this is
worth saying out loud is not our inference alone: \citet{pappu2026multiagent}, optimizing a
prompt with GEPA for an unrelated purpose, report that ``on GPQA Diamond and MATH-500, GEPA
returned the original prompt essentially unchanged'' --- the same event, noticed and reported,
in someone else's pipeline. The second cheap defence, learned the hard way five times over, is
a positive control: an instrument that has never been shown to register a signal cannot be
trusted to report its absence. We release the corrected harness, every cassette and
pre-registration including the superseded ones, and the audit that produced this section, on
the view that a study which invalidates its own headline is more useful published than
withdrawn.

\section{How a Componentized Treatment Can Silently Not Apply}
\label{sec:defect}

Our first fifteen contrast runs reported a $\dd(D-B)$ that was exactly zero ten times and
near-zero or moderately negative otherwise. None was a measurement of the hypothesis. Replaying the flagship run offline, arm~D's winning genome contains a critique
gene \textbf{byte-identical to the seed in $15/15$ cases}: across all arms GEPA changed
\texttt{generate} in $5/15$ winners and \texttt{ensure\_correct} in $0/15$. The two arms whose
difference the study exists to measure were, for the gene that defines the difference, the
same program. Every $\dd$ in Table~\ref{tab:results} is therefore a comparison of a genome
against itself, which is why it was so stable.

Two wiring defects produced that, and neither is visible in a held-out score.
\textbf{The reflective dataset was component-agnostic.} GEPA builds reflection records per
component; ours passed every component the \emph{generator's} inputs and outputs. Asked to
improve a critique gene, the reflector saw drafts and scores but never a verdict the critique
had returned, so it could not observe the behaviour it was editing --- and duly proposed
generation prose into a critique slot, which then lost on training score. \textbf{The
component selector rewrote one component per proposal.} GEPA's default \texttt{round\_robin}
cycles, so under a matched proposal cap the two-gene arm spent half its proposals on each gene
and searched the shared productive gene half as hard as the one-gene arm. A matched
\emph{total} budget is not a matched budget per component, and the confound flatters the
one-gene arm exactly where we were looking for an effect.

Auditing the fix, and then auditing the audit, found the same class of error six more times.
Table~\ref{tab:defects} is the record. Three are worth the reader's attention because they
generalize past our code: a ground-truth signal that is silently \emph{constant} still trains
(defect~6); a replay advertised as strict can swallow a cache miss and exit zero while
reporting a different number (defect~7); and an empty LLM completion accepted as a gene
deletes that step of the loop, which \emph{improves} the training score whenever the step it
deletes is worse than useless (defect~8).

\paragraph{The fix, and the reporting minimum we propose.}
The corrected harness (i) builds a \emph{component-specific} reflective dataset, so the verify
gene's records carry the draft it judged, the verdict it returned, and whether that verdict
was a false pass or a false alarm; (ii) passes an explicit component selector, defaulting to
rewriting all genes per proposal so a multi-gene arm is not starved; (iii) replaces arm~C
with a genome-blind constant draft scored by the real metric, which is flat without being
saturated, and re-measures arm~A instead of copying it; and (iv) records, for every arm, the
per-gene accepted-change count, the train aggregate, and the final gene text. The audit of
that harness (Evidence~6--7) added three more: (v) the verify gene's truth signal now receives
the real task, and a trace that cannot be matched to an evaluated task raises rather than being
scored against a stand-in; (vi) a replay that swallows a cassette miss now fails at context
exit instead of exiting $0$ with a divergent report; and (vii) every run releases the
\emph{per-task} held-out score vector behind each mean, which is what makes a paired
task-level test possible for readers who --- correctly --- do not want to treat our seeds as
replications. Designing the split-size sweep of \S\ref{sec:splitsweep} added an eighth, of the
same shape: (viii) a configuration whose metric-call budget cannot fund a single accept cycle
at the requested train-split size is now \emph{refused} at the dry-run step rather than
priced, because such a run bills in full and reports a flat $\dd$ with
\texttt{genes\_changed=[]} --- a null-shaped result from an arm that never searched. That is
this paper's central failure mode arriving as a \emph{budget} arithmetic slip rather than a
wiring one, which is the argument for making the refusal a property of the harness instead of
a habit of the operator. Running that sweep then produced a ninth (Evidence~9), which is the
same failure mode again from the one direction we were not watching: at
$n_{\mathrm{train}}{=}4$ the \emph{comparator} arm accepted no proposal on any of three seeds,
so its released genome is byte-identical to the seed and $\dd(D-B)$ on that row is really
$\dd(D-\mathrm{seed})$. The guard added as~(viii) does not catch this and cannot: it enforces
that an arm \emph{can} fund an accept cycle, never that the search accepts one. Both are
one-sided contrasts wearing a two-sided label, and the difference is only which arm was
starved --- in the retraction it was the treatment, here the control. The reporting fix is the
same number~(iv) already demands, so what we add is not a guard but a \emph{presentation} rule,
now enforced by the analysis script: \textbf{a $\dd$ between two optimized arms must be
labelled with how many seeds each arm actually accepted on}, and flagged where either is zero.
Sixteen released runs trip that flag. Exactly one is \emph{one-sided} in the new sense, with a
starved comparator facing an arm that did search: the $n_{\mathrm{train}}{=}4$ row. In the other
fifteen \emph{both} arms accepted nothing on every seed, so the flag is re-deriving the original
retraction from the scores alone --- which is the point, since that is the diagnostic we did not
have when we believed them. Those fifteen are reported as uninformative rather than as evidence
of no effect.
Item~(iv) is the part we would urge on others: \textbf{a
componentized-optimization result should report, per component, whether the optimizer's output
actually differs from the seed.} Had we printed that one number, this paper would have been a
different paper fifteen runs earlier. Because
the corrected wiring changes every reflection prompt, it changes every cassette key, so the
released cassettes cannot replay under it; they are retained and labelled superseded. The
first runs recorded under the corrected harness are the two hand-written-gene conditions of
\S\ref{sec:analysis}(1d); they replay bit-for-bit, so \texttt{verify\_reproducibility.py}
verifies eight runs rather than reporting that nothing has been proven --- those two
conditions, the four-arm re-run of \S\ref{sec:rerun}, the two new $p_0$ levels of
\S\ref{sec:p0}, and the three bought rows of the split sweep (\S\ref{sec:splitresult}).
The \emph{discarded first attempt} at the four-arm re-run is not among them,
for a reason worth recording: the blank-gene guard that attempt motivated (Evidence~8) makes
its own cassette unreplayable, since a replay re-serves the empty completion and the guard
raises on it. Its numbers stand as measured and are no longer verifiable under the current
code, which is the honest definition of superseded.

\paragraph{What the \dd\ column does not say.} We report it once, above, and do not
interpret it further. In every run in Table~\ref{tab:results} the loop-level arm's extra gene
was never changed (\S\ref{sec:defect}), so no run here measures the treatment, and the four
exactly-$[0,0]$ intervals are evidence that it was not applied rather than evidence that it
did nothing. We previously called those intervals ``tight, not merely underpowered'' and that
reading is withdrawn.

A second reading needs withdrawing with it, and it is the one \S\ref{sec:decomp} explains. We
described the zeros as \emph{identities} --- arms B and D issuing identical calls, so the
cassette served identical completions. That is too strong, and its falsity is visible in this
very table: run~1 reports $-0.09$ and run~6 $+0.01$, and run~3's per-seed vector is
$[0,+0.055,0,0,-0.055]$, so on several seeds the two arms demonstrably did \emph{not} issue the
same calls. An unchanged verify gene does not make the arms the same program, because they
still differ in the gene they \emph{share}: under the default one-component-per-proposal
selector, arm~D searched \texttt{generate} about half as hard as arm~B (Evidence~3). So the
non-zeros in this column are not residual treatment effects; they are the selector confound
measured through the shared gene, plus sampling noise, which this study can now put a number on.
Of the
fifteen contrast runs recorded under the pre-correction harness, ten have a mean \dd\ of
exactly zero and four have $|\dd| \ge 0.005$, the largest being the powered actuator run at
$-0.100$. The exact zeros are not noise measurements: B and D produced byte-identical genomes
there, so they issued identical calls and the cassette served identical completions. The four
non-zeros are a different matter, and an earlier draft asserted flatly that this study contains
no noise, which \S\ref{sec:threats} now contradicts. Scaled by that $\sigma(\dd)$, two of the
four sit under $1\sigma$ --- the worker swap at $-0.014$ and the cross-family run at $+0.008$ ---
and two do not: the powered actuator run at $\approx\!5\sigma$ and the discarded first four-arm
attempt at $-0.094$, $\approx\!3.5\sigma$. The caveat is that $\sigma$ was estimated on
multi-hard while the largest of these is a code run, so its transfer is an assumption and not a
measurement. This is the same conflation \S\ref{sec:decomp} finds in the corrected re-run, one
harness generation earlier: \dd\ is a whole-system contrast, so it moves when the shared gene
moves, whether or not the treatment ever applied. The corrected-harness measurement of this
contrast is \S\ref{sec:rerun}.

What survives in these runs is the \textbf{arm-A seed baseline}, which is a measurement of
the loop rather than of the optimizer and is untouched by the defect. Read down the ``Seed''
column: it is the readout for runs 6--10, and the next three paragraphs are its story. Run
6's critic is worth one line of it --- routing the critique to \texttt{gpt-5.6-luna} over a
\texttt{gpt-4.1-mini} worker lifted the baseline $0.765 \to 0.825$, directionally consistent
with the reliable-critic hypothesis, but \texttt{gpt-5.6} and \texttt{gpt-4.1} share a vendor
and training lineage, so this is cross-\emph{lineage}, not the cross-\emph{provider} test
that would rule out self-preference bias (D3, \S\ref{sec:directions}).

\paragraph{What we retract.}
The following claims are withdrawn: that there is \emph{no measurable advantage for
loop-level optimization} (the loop-level treatment was not applied); that the null is
\emph{tight rather than underpowered} on the strength of $[0,0]$ intervals (an interval of
$[0,0]$ arises when B and D produce byte-identical genomes, i.e.\ when the arms issued the
same calls and the cassette served the same completions --- the sharpest available evidence
that the experiment did not run, not evidence for its conclusion); that the extra evolvable
gene \emph{over-fits the train split} (the train aggregate was computed and discarded, so
this was untestable from the release, and the selector makes the opposite prediction); that
$\dd(D-B)=-0.10$ \emph{excludes zero}; that this \emph{rules out under-powering}; and any
reading of a BigCodeBench-Hard interval or sign test as \emph{replication across independent
seeds} (Evidence~4b); and that arm~D's $-0.042$ at $L_1$ measures the loop-level treatment,
when the component decomposition of \S\ref{sec:decomp} attributes $-0.025$ of it to the
\texttt{generate} gene both arms evolve.

Two further claims are withdrawn on the evidence of the split sweep and the noise estimate,
both of which post-date the draft that made them. First, \textbf{that evolving the verifier
costs held-out performance at low $p_0$}: the $-0.042$ is $1.49\sigma$ of a measurement noise
we had asserted did not exist, and three other training-split sizes at the same $p_0$ return
$+0.017$, $+0.062$ and $+0.023$ (\S\ref{sec:splitresult}). What replaces it is narrower and is
what the mechanism actually predicts --- the verifier degenerates to non-acceptance at low
$p_0$, on $11/12$ genes across four split sizes --- together with the observation that a
degenerate verifier is not thereby a harmful one, since always-refine is close to the right
policy where almost every draft is wrong. Second, \textbf{that this study contains no
measurement noise}: it contains $\sigma(\dd) = 0.028$ on a thirty-task mean, recoverable at \$0
from released runs, and every interval in the paper omits it (\S\ref{sec:threats}). We also
correct two factual slips: the
reproducibility script registers twelve superseded runs, not ten, and the \$5 ceiling is a CLI
default that four runs overrode (up to \$11.76).
